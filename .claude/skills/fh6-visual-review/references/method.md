# Method: reviewing your own work without fooling yourself

The steps behind the checklist in `SKILL.md`. Numbers match the "method N" tags there. Order matters (it follows Linear Sequential Unmasking from forensic review): freeze, look cold, let a cold reviewer answer, then measure, then open the references, then close with the before/after and the goldens.

## The steps

1. **Freeze the expectations.** Before opening this round's renders, write into the handover: the rank order (gear and shift light, speed, tach, secondary visual, bezel and labels); the feature inventory of the real display, each line with its reference value and tolerance (step 12); the key frames of the sequence (data at the limiter from frame 29 or 30, strobe 30-41, gear change at 42); the blur levels you will use; TJX's rejection patterns (step 3). Answers never edit these later; a changed tolerance is logged with its reason first. ([prediction before hindsight](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5856500/))
2. **Cold look.** Open the full-frame cockpit composite (`hood_02_mid_cruise.png` for a hood-only dash) before the spec, your notes, the board or the references, plus a mirrored copy (`crop --mirror`) and the luminance-only view (`cvd`): changing the form makes your own work look unfamiliar. Write 5 to 10 plain sentences: the elements, the order your eye visits them, every value you can read, the colours, which parts look like lit objects and which look flat. No evaluative words. Describe, then analyse, then judge. ([Feldman's model of criticism](https://us.humankinetics.com/blogs/excerpt/feldman-model-of-criticism))
3. **Name the failure first.** Assume TJX rejected the render and write the likeliest reason. Then check each rejection TJX has already made against a named crop: looks nothing like the real car's display; faint textures on vector shapes or materials that only show at 2x; one slab with blobs stuck on or a backing panel the real display lacks; flat modern widgets instead of the "2027 future" bar; murky, tinted or frosted glass. ([premortem](https://hbr.org/2007/09/performing-a-project-premortem))
4. **One cold reviewer, once per review point.** The `cold-reviewer` agent (`.claude/agents/cold-reviewer.md`) starts in a fresh context. Give it the packet below: neutral file names, the logo zone masked, the display facts, the fixed questions in a fixed order. Not the dash's name, your reasons, your notes or answer options. Record each finding as fixed or not fixed with evidence. Never rerun it or reword its prompt to get a different answer. ([heuristic evaluation](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-conduct-a-heuristic-evaluation/))
5. **Five-second test.** The reviewer looks once at the masked composite, then answers without looking again: what it remembers, what it noticed first, second and third, what the display is for. Compare with the frozen rank order. ([five-second test](https://articles.centercentre.com/?p=85))
6. **Unprompted recognition.** Before any reference is shown: "Which car, maker or display does this cluster come from?" No options, no hints, logo masked. Quote the answer. A generic answer ("a sports car", "a game HUD") is a recognisability finding. Prompting inflates recognition.
7. **Blind glance read.** `visual-review blind mockups/<Name> --seed <n> --anchor cockpit --mask <logo zone>` draws 8 frames at random from the sequence and the states into `mockups/blind/<n>/` (pick a seed whose folder does not exist yet, because threads share `mockups/`, and record it; never hand-pick, never redraw after a bad result). The reader looks at each once, with no zoom and no second look, and writes the gear, the speed, the shift lights (dark, partly lit or all lit) and the rpm zone (low below 50% of the limiter, mid below 80%, high below 97%, limiter above). Compare the gear, speed and zone with the key the tool wrote, and the lights with what that frame shows on a crop (a still shows the strobe in its on or off phase only). ([NHTSA's one-glance interval](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-04-26/html/2013-09883.htm))
8. **Blur ladder.** The glance view and the squint view (`glance`), at both review points on all four states in each view the catalog lists. Elements must fade in reverse rank order, and nothing decorative outlasts the data. ([visual hierarchy](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/visual-hierarchy-ux-definition/))
9. **Contrast per zone.** `glance --zone` for every zone that carries information (gear, speed, lit shift lights, the needle or ribbon head, data labels), in every state and view. ([Design for Driving](https://developers.google.com/cars/design/design-foundations/visual-principles))
10. **Peripheral detection.** With the eyes on the road, the cluster sits 13 to 22 degrees below the gaze. Shift-light stages and the strobe must be large, abrupt changes that stay plain in the squint view. ([peripheral vision in driving](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11073400/))
11. **Crowding round the gear (a finding, not a gate).** Measure the clear space from the gear glyph to the nearest other strong element (`annotate` boxes). Below about 390 px sideways or 420 px above or below (cockpit view), the gear is read with a glance, not peripherally. ([Bouma's law, via Strasburger et al. 2011](http://hans-strasburger.userweb.mwn.de/reprints/peripheral_vision_review.pdf))
12. **Feature inventory, measured.** For each frozen line, the reference value (frontal press image or the maker's documents) beside a value measured on 1:1 crops: number and layout of dials; sweep and start and end angles; needle angle at the shift rpm and the limiter; tick counts (count them on an `unwrap` strip); numeral shapes (1, 4, 7, 0); needle, tip and hub; accent colours (`probe`); LED count and colour order; gear and speed positions as fractions of the cluster width; the signature transitions. Mark each matched, a deliberate change with its reason, or missing. ([Porsche 911 press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911/Interior.html))
13. **Coarse, then fine, both orders.** A sheet of the reference and the render at the same dial or cluster width: first both blurred to the same coarse level (masses, positions, where the light is), then 1:1 detail pairs. Ask every same/different question twice, left and right swapped; a flipped answer is no conclusion, so settle it with step 12. ([coarse-to-fine recognition](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-9280.1994.tb00500.x))
14. **Overlay.** Scale a frontal reference onto the render by the dial centre and diameter (or the cluster width) and compare at 50% and as a difference; the board export shares the 3840x2160 frame, so `diff --fit` and a sheet need no alignment. Perspective owner photos are for materials and behaviour, never for geometry.
15. **Lit objects at glance scale.** At the full-frame scale, each physical part shows shading and a highlight whose position fits its shape and the one key light. A highlight off the shading reads as a white mark, not gloss. Beside the board, the render must look clearly richer. ([highlight congruence](https://doi.org/10.1167/11.9.4))
16. **Glass (glass dashes).** The glass bends what is behind it, highlights move with G, pieces appear by lensing rather than an alpha fade, no glass sits on glass, the glass stays clear. Details in materials.md. ([Meet Liquid Glass, WWDC25](https://developer.apple.com/videos/play/wwdc2025/219/))
17. **Timing sheet.** For each frame: the telemetry (from the key) beside the visual state on 1:1 crops (shift stages lit, strobe on or off, gear glyph, digits, needle angle from hub and tip pixels). Discrete signals change on the data's frame; needles and ribbons trail by at most one frame and never overshoot. The offline sequence shows animation lag only; latency comes from the live bench. ([audio-visual sync](https://www.itu.int/dms_pubrec/itu-r/rec/bt/R-REC-BT.1359-1-199811-I!!PDF-E.pdf))
18. **The strobe behaves like the hardware.** Stages light in the reference's order and colours; the strobe starts on the frame the data reaches the limiter and stops when it leaves; on and off phases are identical whole numbers of frames (whole at both 60 and 120 Hz: 3, 5, 6, 7.5 or 10 Hz). ([shift-light set-up](https://race-technology.com/wiki/index.php/DASH2PROConfiguration/SetUpShiftLights))
19. **Flash safety.** `flash --frames 30-41`. A strobe above 3 flashes a second must keep the concurrently flashing area within 25% of any 10-degree field at 0.6 m: in practice LED-sized lights only. ([WCAG 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html))
20. **Gear change frame by frame.** From 3 frames before to 6 after the gear value changes, on the 1:1 gear crop: the old gear until the data changes, the new gear readable on the first frame after, no blank, doubled or half-faded frame, any morph done within 6 frames (0.1 s feels instant). ([response-time limits](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/response-times-3-important-limits/))
21. **Unexplained change.** `motion` over the sequence, and `diff` on any pair it flags: every region that changes is explained by data (needle, digits, lights, strobe, G-driven highlights). Unchanged digits keep exactly the same pixels. Shimmer, crawling aliasing, bloom popping, boiling noise and a glyph that appears a frame late are findings.
22. **True-black floor.** `stretch` on each state: every non-zero pixel belongs to an intended element or its glow; no rectangle edge of a blur or bloom target, haze or speckle. A 3% ink fill shows as about 19% grey: judge the pixels, not the code values.
23. **Dark gradients.** In the stretched view and 1:1 crops, follow every dark gradient (glass, vignettes, glow tails, ghost segments, off LEDs) for steps or contour lines. ([banding](https://pixelmager.github.io/linelight/banding))
24. **Night legibility.** With an extra blur for a wide pupil, 6, 8, 9 and 0 stay distinct, 1 and 7 stay apart, counters stay open, halos do not join digits. ([light text on dark at a glance](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28166901/))
25. **Glare and static parts.** `static` across the four states and the sequence (four states alone count shared digit strokes as static): parts that never change are dimmer than the live data and hold no large near-white area; they are also what an OLED's image-retention care dims. Read its area list, not only the p95. ([LG OLED care](https://www.lg.com/hk_en/tv/oled-tv/oled-reliability/))
26. **Before and after.** The kept before beside the final, full frame and 1:1, with `diff`. Write the before's main failure in one sentence and check it is gone; then name what the before did better and check it is not lost. ([considering the opposite](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.47.6.1231))
27. **Goldens are images.** After `FH6_UPDATE_GOLDEN=1`, open every golden PNG and check it like a render. Every difference from the previous goldens is an intended change; an unexplained one is fixed in the code, never accepted by rewriting the golden.

The direction check is `SKILL.md`'s procedure steps 1 to 6. They run steps 1 to 10 here, the layout, geometry and colour lines of step 12, steps 13 to 16, and the measurements of that procedure's step 4 on the first build: the flash audit (18, 19), motion (21), the black floor and dark gradients (22, 23), static parts (25) and the diff against the before render (26). They answer the lines marked DC. The closing review runs all 27, adding the timing sheet (17), the gear change (20), night legibility (24) and the goldens (27).

## The cold-reviewer packet

Make neutral copies in a folder that does not name the dash, with the logo zone masked (take it from your `Geom` table and check it on a `crop --mask` first):

```bash
VR="cargo run --release -p visual-review --"
$VR blind mockups/<Name> --seed <n> --anchor cockpit --mask <logo zone X,Y,W,H>   # mockups/blind/<n>/glance_01.png ... glance_08.png
$VR crop mockups/<Name>/02_mid_cruise_cockpit.png --view cockpit --anchor cockpit --mask <logo zone> --out mockups/blind/<n>/A.png
cp mockups/references/<Name>/<photo>.jpg mockups/blind/<n>/R1.jpg                  # one neutral name per reference photo
```

Run `blind` first: it refuses a folder that already holds anything. For a hood-only dash use `hood_02_mid_cruise.png --view hood --anchor hood` for A.png and `--anchor hood` for `blind`, with the mask in hood-local coordinates.

Start the `cold-reviewer` agent once with only these paths and this brief (fill in the paths; change nothing else). A session that does not list that agent (it started before the agent existed) starts `general-purpose` instead, with the text of `.claude/agents/cold-reviewer.md` above the brief:

> You will look at images of a car instrument cluster made for a 55-inch 4K OLED (0.317 mm pixels) in a sim-racing cockpit, seen from 0.6 to 0.9 m. Off pixels are true black. A driver reads it in quick glances away from the road. Answer in this order, once, without revising an earlier answer. Give findings and yes/no answers only: no scores, ratings or grades.
> 1. Open A.png, look once, then answer without looking again: what do you remember; what did you notice first, second and third; what is the display for?
> 2. Which car, maker or display does this cluster come from? Say "don't know" if you are not sure.
> 3. For each of glance_01.png to glance_08.png: look once, with no zoom and no second look, then write the gear, the speed, the shift lights (dark, partly lit or all lit) and the rpm zone (low below 50% of the limiter, mid below 80%, high below 97%, limiter above).
> 4. Only now open R1.jpg to Rn.jpg (photos of a real display), with A.png open again beside each. For each, is the layout of the cluster in A.png the same or different? Then answer again with the two images named the other way round.
> 5. List anything that looks wrong, cheap, flat, illegible, or unlike a real car's display, each with the image and where in it.

Keep the key (`mockups/<Name>/review/blind_key_<n>.txt`) and your notes away from the agent. If no fresh-context agent can be started, label the step "self-audit" in the handover, not a gate result: V3, V4, V5 and V13 are then NOT ASSESSED for a missing input, which blocks a "go" and the close, so ask the coordinator for a session that can start one before deciding the direction check or closing the review.

## Pitfalls

- Claude never sees a 4K frame 1:1. Rims, ticks and 1 to 2 px labels vanish or alias in a full frame: judge detail on 1:1 crops. ([Claude vision](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/vision))
- Counting many small objects, reading needle angles and judging geometry by eye are where vision models fail. Measure: `unwrap`, the protractor, `probe`. ([VLMs are blind](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.06581); [reading clocks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05092))
- Models favour their own output, and self-correction without outside feedback does not reliably help. Use the tools and one fresh reviewer, not more self-reflection. ([self-preference](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.13076); [self-correction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.01798))
- Judges lean towards one side in side-by-side questions: ask both ways round. ([LLM-as-a-judge biases](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05685))
- A tolerance picked after seeing the render turns every miss into a match: freeze it first.
- A multi-digit speed cannot be read 15 to 22 degrees below the gaze without a glance; test the speed with the blind read and the shift light with the squint view.
- Every saturated-red flash counts toward WCAG 2.3.1 whatever its luminance.
- Keep the blur levels and the blind-read size the same between rounds, or before/after comparisons are not like for like.
- Never upload renders or references to online font identifiers, contrast checkers or flash checkers.
