# PorscheAnalog research: the Porsche 911 (992) five-tube cluster

Step 1 of `program/phases/07-cluster-factory/CLUSTER_PROMPT.md`. Researched on 2026-11-27 from Porsche's own newsroom, press kits, model pages and owner's manuals, plus reviews and owner forums (marked as such). Every point below names its source. Where a source does not say something, the gap is listed at the end rather than guessed.

## What this dash recreates

**The Porsche 911 of the 992 generation: its "five-tube" instrument cluster with the rev counter in the middle.** Two versions exist and the dash takes from both:

- **992.1 (2019 to 2024):** a physical analogue rev counter in the centre, flanked by two frameless seven-inch "freeform" displays that draw the other round dials ([Porsche, "What's the clock?"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/products/porsche-911-rev-counter-analogue-eight-generations-992-timeless-machine-valencia-16966.html); [Porsche, 992 world premiere](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/products/porsche-911-new-eigth-generation-992-timeless-machine-design-icon-world-premiere-los-angeles-2018-16522.html); [992.1 GT3 RS release, "the two 7-inch side displays"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2022/products/porsche-911-gt3-rs-world-premiere-29177.html)).
- **992.2 (2024 on, current production):** a fully digital 12.6-inch curved display (12.65 inches on the model-year 2027 pages) with up to seven views, including a 911-only Classic view with "five round dials" and a central rev counter, and a rev-counter mode whose scale is turned so the needle stands at 12 o'clock at the shift speed, "as in many historic Porsche racing cars" ([911 interior press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911/Interior.html); German kit: "Fünftuben-Design", needle "etwa in 12-Uhr-Stellung" at the shift rpm, [Interieur](https://newsroom.porsche.com/de/pressemappen/911/Interieur.html); [911 Carrera model page](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/carrera-models/911-carrera/)).

Porsche itself calls the round positions **tubes**: the 992 owner's manual labels "Speed & Assist" (tubes 1 and 2), the rev counter in the middle, and "Car & Info" (tubes 4 and 5) ([Driver's Manual WKD 992, p. 122](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=122#manual); [MY22 911 Quick Start Guide](https://contentful-assets.porsche.com/u1ih0u5p8hpf/3JEFEDas1PeDALj7ccO2xy/749db159ca9bb96ca7344e2627640bbf/Porsche_MY22_911_QSG.pdf)).

The centred rev counter is Porsche's oldest instrument signature: it comes from the 550 Spyder of 1953, moved to the 356 A in 1955 and has been in every 911 since the 901 of 1963. The 550, 356 and early 912 had three round instruments; the 911 has five ([Christophorus 417, "Icon"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/christophorus/en/2025/417/icon.html); [Porsche, centred tachometer history](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/history/porsche-icon-christophorus-417-tachometer-41697.html)).

## What makes it recognisable

### Layout
1. **Five round dials in a row, the rev counter the largest, in the middle.** On the 992.2 Classic view the five dials **overlap: each outer dial is tucked behind its neighbour**, largest at the centre. On the GT3 Touring they are, left to right, a voltmeter, the speedometer, the rev counter, an oil pressure and temperature dial, and a coolant dial ([911 GT3 Touring model page, cluster close-up](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/911-gt3-models/911-gt3-touring/); [Turbo S model page](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/911-turbo-models/911-turbo-s/)). In the 992.1 the outer two are only partly visible; a review notes the wheel rim hid them ([The Autopian](https://www.theautopian.com/its-time-everyone-stopped-freaking-out-over-the-new-porsche-911s-digital-gauge-cluster/)), and on the 992.2 the outer dials "curve into a semi-circle" so all five stay in view (same review).
2. **The tubes sit low and recessed** under a horizontal dashboard wing; the side screens are frameless, with their glass edges exposed so they appear to float ([992 Valencia press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:3e6d394d-ba34-4103-838c-de0d94344410/PAG_Valencia_PM_EN.pdf)).
3. **What the tubes hold:** the left side has speed (a speedometer dial, 0 to 330 km/h on the 992.1 and 30 to 360 km/h on the GT3 Touring); the right side holds Car & Info items, among them **G-Force: current and peak longitudinal and lateral acceleration in a circular plot**, a power and torque view, and a boost display on some variants ([manual p. 127](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=127#manual), [p. 128](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=128#manual), [p. 123](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=123#manual)).

### The rev counter
4. **Black face, upright white numerals, a thick white bar at every 1,000 rpm and dense fine ticks between them.** In Porsche's own close-up of the 992.1 dial the numerals are 0 to 8, about 30° per 1,000 rpm, 0 near 8 o'clock and 8 near 4 o'clock (a 240° sweep), with 4 at 12 o'clock ([Porsche press photo "911 Carrera 4S (992), rev counter"](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2019/products/porsche-911-rev-counter-analogue-eight-generations-992-timeless-machine-valencia-16966.html), studied at full size). The GT3 dial reads to 10 at about 24° per 1,000 rpm ([GT3 model page](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/911-gt3-models/911-gt3/); [GT3 2021 premiere](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2021/products/porsche-911-gt3-world-premiere-livestream-23624.html)).
5. **The red zone is a red band on the outer edge or red minor ticks**, starting at the maximum permitted engine speed; the numerals stay white ([Quick Start Guide, p. 8](https://contentful-assets.porsche.com/u1ih0u5p8hpf/3JEFEDas1PeDALj7ccO2xy/749db159ca9bb96ca7344e2627640bbf/Porsche_MY22_911_QSG.pdf); GT3 page: solid red band from 9 to 10; owner report of red minor ticks from 7,400 rpm on a Carrera: [Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/992/1321244-redline-night-vs-day.html)).
6. **"1/min x 1000"** is printed on the face (Carrera 4S press photo; Turbo S page, under the "Turbo S" script).
7. **The needle is a slim pointer on a central round hub cap with a short tail past the hub**: white with a polished silver domed cap on the 992.1 Carrera; yellow on the GT3, orange-red on the Turbo S, white on the rotated GTS dial of the 992.2 (Carrera 4S press photo; [GT3](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/911-gt3-models/911-gt3/), [Turbo S](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/911-turbo-models/911-turbo-s/) and [Carrera GTS](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/carrera-models/911-carrera-gts/) model pages). The S/T and Heritage cars have white hands with silver caps ([911 S/T design](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911-s-t/Design-and-equipment.html)).
8. **A darker inner zone** inside the numeral ring holds the telltales above the hub and a window below it with the digital speed and the gear (Carrera 4S press photo; manual: a "digital speedometer" in the tach shows speed, selector position and the engaged gear, [p. 122](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=122#manual)).
9. **Model lettering in the upper dial:** "GT3" script (evo calls it a first), "Turbo S" script, "GTS" in the contrast colour ([evo, 992.1 GT3 buying guide](https://www.evo.co.uk/porsche/911-gt3/203766/used-porsche-911-gt3-9921-2021-2024-review-history-specs-and-buying-guide); [911 GTS premiere](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2021/products/porsche-911-gts-models-premiere-24875.html); model pages).
10. **The rotated scale (992.2 rev-counter mode).** Porsche puts the shift speed at about 12 o'clock (the US kit says redline; the GT3 kit says the 9,000 rpm limit sits "in the traditional position or at the 12 o'clock mark") ([911 GT3 cockpit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911-GT3/Cockpit-and-interior.html); [US Carrera kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/dam/jcr:928354fb-31e2-4b4c-af93-2c22d6de07b4/US%20911%20Carrera%20and%20Carrera%20GTS%20Press%20Kit.pdf); [evo, 992.2 GT3](https://evo.co.uk/porsche/911-gt3/207572/porsche-911-gt3-9922-2025-review-the-best-gt3-yet)). On the rotated Carrera GTS dial, 0 sits near 5 o'clock, the numerals climb clockwise through the bottom and up the left side, the red band starts at 12, and **the sector from about 1 to 5 o'clock carries no numerals: the digital readout sits there, right of the hub** ([Carrera GTS model page](https://www.porsche.com/international/models/911/carrera-models/911-carrera-gts/)). Racers turn the tach so the shift point is at 12 to read rpm in peripheral vision ([Rennlist, owners](https://rennlist.com/forums/racing-and-drivers-education-forum/196280-rotating-the-tach.html)).

### Shift lights
11. **GT3: coloured bars left and right of the rev counter plus a motorsport shift light** ([GT3 2021 premiere](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2021/products/porsche-911-gt3-world-premiere-livestream-23624.html)); a ride report describes "two arcing lights either side of the rev counter" lighting as revs rise ([CarExpert](https://www.carexpert.com.au/car-reviews/2021-porsche-911-gt3-review-prototype-ride-along)). The 992.2 Carrera T has "shift lights arranged around the digital tach" ([Jalopnik](https://jalopnik.com/2025-porsche-911-carrera-t-is-perfectly-tailored-for-di-1851702446)).
12. **Carrera gear shift assist (manual gearbox):** segments fill white, then yellow, then red as the shift point nears; **when every segment is red a "+" appears next to the engaged gear** ([manual p. 239](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=239#manual)).

### Colour, light and type
13. **Night light is white**; the numerals and ticks are lit through the face, and on the 992.1 the tick marks are separate white and red strips behind the face ([Rennlist, night drive](https://rennlist.com/forums/992/1324455-first-night-drive-blinded-by-the-interior-light.html); [Rennlist, tach face DIY](https://rennlist.com/forums/diy-992/1333818-tachometer-gauge-face-replacement-diy.html); [Rennlist, numeral back-lighting](https://rennlist.com/forums/992/1239150-is-there-a-way-to-change-the-color-of-the-tach-numbers-so-you-can-see-them.html)).
14. **Colour options are part of the identity:** coloured rev-counter dials (Guards Red, Carmine Red, Slate Grey Neo, Turbonite, Racing Yellow, Arctic Blue), and the **Heritage look: green numerals with white needles**, a 356 and 1963 911 tribute, on the S/T, Sport Classic, Targa 4S Heritage Design Edition and Spirit 70 ([911 GT3 configurator, "Coloured instrument dials"](https://configurator.porsche.com/en-CW/mode/model/992812); [911 interior press kit](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/911/Interior.html); [Targa 4S Heritage Design Edition](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2020/products/porsche-exclusive-manufaktur-911-targa-heritage-design-edition-992-eighth-generation-21067.html); [911 Spirit 70](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2025/products/porsche-911-spirit-70-39294.html)).
15. **Type:** Porsche's corporate face is Porsche Next (from 2017); no source confirms the dial numerals use it. The numerals are an upright, low-contrast grotesque: a flagged 1, a closed 4, a straight 7 (Carrera 4S press photo; [Porsche design system, typography](https://designsystem.porsche.com/v4/must-know/typography/)). Porsche Next is not freely licensed, so the dash uses Barlow Semi Condensed (SIL OFL, the family's `dial` role), the closest open match: a slightly rounded, low-contrast grotesque ([Barlow](https://github.com/jpt/barlow)).

### Behaviour
16. **The cluster greets the driver by profile** ("Hallo FAHRER 1" in the German UI of the press photo; profiles are switched in the right display) (Carrera 4S press photo; [manual p. 124](https://www.manualslib.com/manual/1870968/Porsche-911.html?page=124#manual)).
17. **2026 to 2027 Porsche: the rev counter returns as a rendered instrument.** The model-year 2027 Taycan's E-Shift adds a virtual rev counter (to 7,500 rpm), a gear indicator with a shift light, and an animated needle that "quivers gently" even at standstill ([Taycan model-year innovations](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/2026/products/porsche-model-year-innovations-taycan-42641.html); [Christophorus, E-Shift](https://newsroom.porsche.com/christophorus/en/2026/420/e-shift.html)). The Cayenne Electric's 14.25-inch OLED keeps the "tube layout" with a round power meter in the centre ([Cayenne Electric Driver Experience](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Cayenne-Electric-and-Cayenne-Coup%C3%A9-Electric/Porsche-Driver-Experience.html)), and the Mission X concept turns a curved cluster towards the driver with a Track mode ([Mission X interior](https://newsroom.porsche.com/en/press-kits/Porsche-Mission-X/Interior-design-and-the-Driver-Experience-(DX).html)).

## Deliberate changes and why

- **A. Contents of the outer and right tubes.** The real dials show voltage, oil pressure and temperature, and coolant; Forza's telemetry carries none of them. The tubes show only verified fields instead: G-Force (a real Car & Info view), power (from `power_watts`) and boost (from `boost_psi`, a real display on some variants). Nothing is invented.
- **B. The gear is the hero.** In the real car the engaged gear is a small digit in the readout window. Our rank order puts gear and shift light first, so the window is larger and the gear sits under a sapphire magnifier in it, like a watch's date window under its cyclops lens. The window stays in the scale's dead zone, where Porsche puts its readout, so the needle never crosses it.
- **C. The 2027 step.** Beyond the 992.2, the dash renders the tubes as physical hardware (machined bezels, a spun inner zone, a lacquered needle on a satin cap with its light leaking round it, smoked lenses) lit by one light that swings with lateral G, under glass computed from `docs/specs/optics.md` (exact Fresnel reflection, Snell refraction at the crystal's edge, a magnifying dome over the gear). When a different car is detected the scale turns to put that car's shift rpm at 12 o'clock, a mechanism no production cluster has.
- **D. No speed-limit sign, telltales or fuel.** These need data the game does not send.
- **E. The brief's two-rate scale** (shift to limiter magnified four times) is replaced by Porsche's real rotated linear scale.
- **F. The brief's jewel visor above the dial** is replaced by the GT3's shift bars, set into the bezel round the dial: eleven lenses within 65° of 12 o'clock, so on the track face they lie only over the approach to the shift point and the red zone, never beside the low numerals (the direction check's cold reviewer read a ring all round the top as running against the scale).
- **G. The scale's top.** Porsche ends the face at a whole thousand above the maximum engine speed (8 on the 7,500 rpm Carrera, 10 on the 9,000 rpm GT3). The dash takes the first whole thousand at least 500 rpm above the limiter, so the red band always reads as a band (8,700 → 10, as the GT3).
- **H. Small readouts refresh like a car display.** Power, boost and the G value take a new value at 5 Hz with hysteresis, and the needle holds the peak of a bounce off the limiter, as a damped needle does; nothing flickers on the limiter (WCAG 2.3.1 and the strobe rule). The power and boost scales are printed for the car: their tops follow the highest value the car has shown in a race, in whole steps of the user's unit, and change only while the driver is off the throttle, so a scale never re-ranges mid-pull.
- **I. The logo sits below the hub on the track face.** On the 992.2 the model lettering is above the hub, but on the track face the needle stands at 12 o'clock at the shift point, so lettering above the hub would sit under the needle exactly when the driver looks for the shift. The dash puts the logo and "1/min × 1000" below the hub whenever they clear the gear window there (full width, else a narrower zone a little lower; the needle only passes it near idle). That covers the common limiters at the default shift point; where the window sits low (a short shift point, or a limiter just past a half thousand) and on the classic face, the logo goes above the hub, where the 992.2 has its badge.
- **M. One grotesque for every figure.** The tokens give the gear and the speed the display role (Saira Condensed); this dash sets them, like the dial numerals, in Barlow Semi Condensed, the closest open match to Porsche's dial face, so every figure on the cluster shares one typeface. The user's display font still replaces all of them.
- **J. The outer tubes keep their size.** On the 992.2 the outer tubes are about two thirds of the rev counter's height; here they are 0.44, so the five tubes fit the cockpit envelope at the anchor with the rev counter at full size. Tucking the side tubes 36 px behind the rev counter shows more of each outer tube.
- **K. Bezels, hub and needle colour.** The 992.1's rev counter bezel is the darkest ring of the dial and its hub cap black; the dash's bezels are dark graphite titanium with polished inner chamfers and its hub cap black satin chrome (builds 3 to 5 used light titanium, which three reviewers read as aftermarket chrome). The needles take the palette's primary, so they match the car's brand colour in factory mode; the white needles of the 992.1 are the Heritage option.
- **L. Sizes against the tokens.** The tokens ask for a hero gear cap of at least 150 px and a speed within 420 px of it. Here the gear is cap 96 (about 101 through the low magnifier, which enlarges about 5 %) and the speed digits sit about 510 px from it, because the 992 layout puts the speed in its own tube; the gear still reads first because it is the only large figure in the rev counter, under the magnifier, and the shift lenses and "+" sit round it. The tokens' 0/2/4 px G parallax is replaced by the light swing: the tubes are fixed hardware, and the light moving across them is what a real cluster does under lateral load.

## Gaps (not verified)
- No source gives the 992.2 shift-light segment count, colour order or flash behaviour; one 2021 forum report describes the 992.1 GT3's as yellow rising to flashing blue ([Rennlist](https://rennlist.com/forums/991-gt3-gt3rs-gt2rs-and-911r/1124433-991-2-gt3-touring-tpms-irace-circuitn-mode-2.html)). The dash uses the palette's stages, and only three lenses at 12 o'clock strobe (the 42 px strobe rule).
- The rpm at 12 o'clock in the rotated mode is not published ("about" the shift speed); the dash uses the shift point the OSD sets (`ShiftPointPct`).
- The dial's material and the cover's shape are not documented; an owner calls the cover clear plastic. The dash uses a flat crystal with a narrow rounded edge, computed by the kit.
- The typeface of the dial numerals is not named by any source.
- The racing cars Porsche means by "many historic Porsche racing cars" are not named.
