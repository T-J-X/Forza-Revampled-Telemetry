- **Category:** Supercar · **Target:** `inspired` · **Kind:** new · **View:** cockpit
- **Family:** Classic Analog (Design Council 3B), spec [docs/design/families/classic-analog.md](../../../../../docs/design/families/classic-analog.md), hero frames [docs/design/heroes/classic-analog/](../../../../../docs/design/heroes/classic-analog/)

**Inspired by the Porsche 911 GT3 RS (992), taken out of this world** (TJX, 2026-11-28: "the gt3 rs inspired would look dope with an out of this world design"). `PorscheAnalog` already recreates the 992 five-tube cluster, so this dash does not repeat it. It keeps what an owner would recognise from the GT3 RS and rebuilds each part as a spaceflight instrument. From the car, as the research note of 2026-11-28 found it in Porsche's own material (check it again in step 1): the 992.1 GT3 RS has one physical rev counter between two 7-inch screens, with 5,000 rpm at 12 o'clock, a 9,000 rpm limit and a small digital window for speed and gear; a Track Screen that cuts the two screens down to tyre pressure, oil pressure, oil temperature, water temperature and fuel level; a shift assistant of two yellow bars beside the rev counter that fill with the revs and flash blue at the shift point; and four rotary controls on the wheel. On the 992.2 the scale can be turned so that the 9,000 rpm limit stands at 12 o'clock, which is the idea this dash is built on. From spaceflight: the Apollo attitude ball (black on one side, red at the poles, yellow director needles), an orbit map and docking lights.

Starting layout, three detached parts. Rank 3: a Ø500 rev counter at (0, −20) with no hub, where the needle is a small craft riding the rim and the red zone ends at 12 o'clock, the apogee. Rank 1: the gear (Saira Condensed 800, cap 190) in the dial centre, which stands for the planet, and 12 docking lights on a straight strip above it (760 × 30), filling outside-in. Rank 2: tabular speed (Saira Condensed 600, cap 96) under the gear inside the dial. Rank 4, left: a Ø260 attitude ball, a real 3D sphere in a gimbal cage. Rank 4, right: a 300 × 300 transfer plot, where each gear is a circular orbit and the craft flies the ellipse between this gear's orbit and the next. Rank 5: mission-clock style micro-labels and the Porsche crest.

- **Signature moment:** The transfer burn. Through a pull the craft climbs its transfer ellipse, brightest at the low point where it is fastest, and reaches apogee exactly at the shift rpm, where the rim marker stands at 12 o'clock. On the upshift the ellipse closes into the next gear's circle in 300 ms on the `fast` spring and a new ellipse opens from it. Two thin yellow bars beside the dial fill with the revs, as the car's shift assistant does, and stay steady. On the limiter only the docking lights strobe, and the apogee marker holds a steady `shift` colour; the car flashes its bars blue, and the handover records the steady bars as a deliberate change (the house rule keeps the flash on the shift strip). The attitude ball shows a drift as a pilot would see it: the nose marker stays centred while the prograde marker, where the car is really going, slides away by the slip angle.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → rim craft, docking lights, limiter and the craft's place on the ellipse (true anomaly θ = π × the rpm fraction between idle and shift, so the plot repeats the rev counter and cannot disagree with it); gear → centre numeral and the active orbit; per-gear speed ÷ rpm learned live into a fixed `[f32; 10]` table → orbit radii and the readout "speed at shift"; speed → tabular speed; acceleration (felt force) → ball pitch and roll on the `gBall` spring; local velocity → slip angle β = atan2(v_x, v_z) → prograde marker; throttle → plume brightness. Orbit shapes are computed, never drawn by hand: r(θ) = a(1 − e²) / (1 + e·cos θ) with a = (r₁ + r₂) / 2 and e = (r₂ − r₁) / (r₂ + r₁), and brightness along the arc follows the vis-viva speed v² ∝ 2/r − 1/a. Pitch, roll and yaw from the packet are not verified and are not used.
- **References:** Porsche 911 GT3 RS (992) instrument cluster, Track Screen and shift assistant; the Apollo flight director attitude indicator and the DSKY; Hohmann transfer diagrams; the orbit view of mission-control displays; `PorscheAnalog` in this catalog for the rendered-dial method
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the rev counter's ring as flight hardware (machined titanium, safety-wired fasteners, a ceramic-white index ring), the attitude ball as a textured sphere in its cage (glTF for the kit's mesh pass, with a sprite fallback), and the docking-light caps. ComfyUI: crinkled gold insulation foil for the dial's backing, a heat-shield tile surface for the side parts, and a faint star-field environment plate for reflections. MSDF atlases for Saira Condensed 600 and 800, B612 Mono and Barlow Semi Condensed 500.
- **Risk:** Two ideas in one dash can end as a costume: every space part must do a driving job (the ball is the G and slip display, the plot is the shift coach), and anything that does neither is cut. The transfer plot must never be read as a map or as real navigation. `GeodesicWell` already owns the gravity-well lattice, so no warped grid here. A rotating 3D ball can pull the eye: it moves only with real forces and sits at rank 4 luminance. Perf: one mesh of about 2,000 triangles, two ellipses in chunks of 8 segments, est. 0.25 ms.
