**An endless tiling inside a circle, shown twice.** In the hyperbolic plane a regular tiling goes on for ever, and the whole of it fits inside a disc: the tiles shrink toward the rim and never reach it, as in Escher's Circle Limit prints. The same tiling can be drawn in two ways. In Poincaré's disc every edge is an arc that meets the rim at a right angle and every angle is true. In Klein's disc every edge is a straight chord. The dash shows both, side by side, and the car moves through the tiling: the felt force slides the plane under the disc, by an exact motion that keeps every tile the same size in its own geometry. Step 1 takes the formulas from a textbook source and checks them against the maths table.

Starting layout, mirrored about the centre line. Rank 4: two discs, Ø400 at (±430, −20). The left is Poincaré's and the right is Klein's, and both show the same tiling at the same moment. Rank 1: the gear (Exo 2 800, cap 170) at (0, −70) on true black between the discs, and ten lamps on a straight line at y −296 (760 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 96) under the gear at (0, +110). Rank 3: the rim of each disc is a lit arc of 270°, the two mirrored, filling from the bottom up the outer side; Barlow Semi Condensed numerals stand on the left disc only. Rank 5: one small label under each disc naming the model, and the logo zone.

- **Signature moment:** The slide. At rest the middle tile sits in the middle of each disc. The felt force translates the plane by 1.2 units of hyperbolic length for each g, so at 1 g the tile that was in the middle sits 0.537 of the way to the rim in Poincaré's disc and 0.834 of the way in Klein's: the tiles slide toward the side the car is pushed, shrink as they near the rim, new ones grow out of the opposite rim, and the tiling holds there while the force holds. Nothing ever crosses the rim. The gear chooses the tiling: polygons of (gear + 2) sides, triangles in first and one more side for each gear after it, with the fewest meeting at a corner that this geometry allows (seven triangles, five squares, four pentagons, four hexagons, then three). Neutral and reverse show first gear's tiling. On a change the old tiling fades over 150 ms. With a steady force, nothing moves. On the limiter only the lamps strobe, and both rims hold a steady `shift` colour.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max with the car's per-gear thresholds → the rim arcs, the lamps and the limiter; gear → the numeral and the tiling; speed → the tabular speed; acceleration → the translation of the plane (direction and length); throttle → the brightness of the tile edges. Every one of these is a verified field.
- **References:** M. C. Escher's Circle Limit prints and Coxeter's account of their geometry; the Poincaré and Beltrami–Klein models of the hyperbolic plane; `GeodesicWell` in this catalog for geometry driven by the felt force
- **Phase 6 assets:** Procedural: each tiling's corners are computed once at load, out to the tiles that reach 92% of the radius, and every frame they are put through the translation and redrawn, as arcs on the left and as chords on the right. Blender: the two rims as thin machined rings, rendered at 4K. ComfyUI: a fine etched-glass grain for the disc faces, used as a labelled sparkle map. Penpot: the board with both discs at rest and at 1 g. MSDF atlases for Exo 2 300 and 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 500.
- **Risk:** Tiles shrink without end at the rim and would shimmer: no tile narrower than 6 px is drawn, and the outer 8% of each disc fades to black. Two discs can read as two dials: neither has a needle or a scale across its face, and the rev arc is on the rims only. A pattern that streams sideways can pull the eye in a corner: the edges are no brighter than 30% of the gear. Perf: at most about 230 edges in each disc, drawn as arcs on the left and lines on the right, est. 0.25 ms.
