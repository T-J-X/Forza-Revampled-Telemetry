**A twist on the Nissan GT-R Nismo (R35) and its Multi Function Display** (TJX, 2026-11-28: Skyline and Nismo twists, "some retro and some modern"; in the game as the 2020 and 2024 Nissan GT-R Nismo). Partly found by the check of 2026-11-28 (Nissan's launch release of October 2007: "a large center-mounted tachometer, with gear display on its upper right"; the 2024 press kit: a red-accented tachometer on the Nismo), the rest recalled and to confirm in step 1 from the press kits and the MFD owner's manuals: a three-dial cluster ruled by that tachometer, and a centre-console Multi Function Display whose graphics were made with Polyphony Digital, the Gran Turismo studio, on which the driver builds pages of virtual gauges: boost, pedal positions, steering angle, a G plot and time graphs. The twist: the driver's own page is the dash. The tachometer stays in the middle and four slots around it take whichever gauges the driver assigns.

Starting layout, detached parts. Rank 3: a Ø460 tachometer at (0, −20) as a rendered dial with a machined, chronograph-style ring, scaled from `EngineMaxRpm`. Rank 1: the gear (Saira Condensed 800, cap 170) in a window at the dial's upper right, as on the car, with a straight row of 12 lamps above the dial (740 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 2: tabular speed (Saira Condensed 600, cap 96) inside the dial's lower half. Rank 4: four gauge slots, each 250 × 200, two on each side of the dial; by default boost and the G plot on the left, pedals and steering on the right. Each slot draws its value the way the MFD does: a small round virtual dial, a horizontal bar, a cross-hair plot or a scrolling time graph. Rank 5: the page name and the Nismo logo.

- **Signature moment:** The custom page. The four slots are the driver's: in the OSD each takes any gauge from the list, and the dash keeps the page. On screen the slots share one light and one grid, so a page always looks designed. At the shift rpm the tachometer's ring takes a steady `shift` colour and the gear window's frame lights; on the limiter only the lamp row strobes. On an upshift the gear digit changes at once, as the real one does, with a 1.12 → 1.0 entry and no roll.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → tachometer needle, ring, lamp row and limiter; gear → the window; speed → tabular speed. The gauge list holds only what the packet carries, and the real MFD's list happens to match it well: boost, throttle, brake, steering angle, longitudinal G, lateral G and the two as a cross-hair plot, power, and the acceleration timers from speed and timestamp. The slot choices are `config.extra` keys named `GtrNismoMfdSlot1` to `GtrNismoMfdSlot4`, parsed when the config changes. Oil and transmission temperatures and pressures, the torque split and fuel flow are on the real list and not in the telemetry: they are not offered.
- **References:** Nissan GT-R (R35) Multi Function Display owner's manuals and press kits, including the 2017 facelift; the Gran Turismo telemetry screens by the same studio; the GT-R Nismo cluster with its red tachometer; `Gt7ModularTripod` in this catalog for the paged-panel idea
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the tachometer with its machined ring and the gear window, and one shared slot frame, with three light azimuths. ComfyUI: a carbon-look meter surround as on the Nismo, and the fine pattern of the display face. MSDF atlases for Saira Condensed 600 and 800, Barlow Semi Condensed 500 and Titillium Web 600. Procedural fallback for every part.
- **Risk:** A gear at the upper right of a dial is off the centreline, against the rule that the hero gear leads on X = 1920: either the window is large and bright enough to win the glance where the car has it, or the gear moves to the dial centre and the handover says why. Four free slots can produce an ugly page: every gauge is drawn on the same 250 × 200 grid and none blooms. The real graphics must come from the manuals and frames, not from memory. Perf: one dial plate and four small gauges, est. 0.2 ms.
