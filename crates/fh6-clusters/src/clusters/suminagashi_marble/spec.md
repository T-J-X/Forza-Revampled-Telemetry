**Japanese floating ink, for a game set in Japan.** Suminagashi is the oldest marbling: ink is touched to still water, each drop pushes the earlier ones outward into rings, and a breath or a fan bends the rings before paper lifts the print. Its motion has an exact description, known as mathematical marbling: every drop and every stroke is a closed-form map that keeps areas, because water does not compress. Step 1 researches the craft from makers' and museums' own pages and takes the maps from the published papers. Here the ink is light on black water.

Starting layout: a wide tray of black water, 1240 × 330 at (0, +20), in a lacquered rim. Rank 1: the gear (Exo 2 800, cap 160) on a black stone, Ø200, in the middle of the tray, and ten lamps on a straight line above the tray at y −185 (760 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 4: the ink rings round the stone. Their edges lie at √(100² + k × 90²) px from the middle, so the first is at 135 px and the tenth at 302 px, and every ring holds the same area of ink. The tray's edge cuts the widest rings, as a real tray does. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 90) on a second stone, 260 × 130 at (+420, +20). A third stone at (−420, +20) shows the felt force as a number. Rank 3: the tray's lower rim is a light bar, 1100 px long, lit from the left, with Barlow Semi Condensed numerals under it.

- **Signature moment:** One ring for each gear. A drop of ink grows at the stone as the revs climb, its area linear in the revs from the rpm at which the gear was entered to the shift point, so every ring already on the water is pushed outward, smoothly and exactly: a point at distance ρ from the middle moves to √(ρ² + r²) when a drop of radius r arrives. At the shift point the drop is a full ring, and the upshift keeps it (the ring areas follow their targets on the `snap` spring, so a downshift drains the newest ring back into the stone): in sixth gear five full rings and one growing ring lie on the water. The felt force combs them: one stroke across the tray moves every point along the force by z × 2^(−s ÷ c), where s is the point's distance from the stroke's line, c is 90 px and z is 60 px for each g. A corner drags the rings into a feather that points the way the car is pushed, and braking drags it forward. With steady revs and no force, nothing moves. On the limiter only the lamps strobe, and the newest ring holds a steady `shift` colour.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max with the car's per-gear thresholds → the growing drop, the rim bar, the lamps and the limiter; gear → the numeral and the count of full rings; speed → the tabular speed; acceleration → the two combing strokes (lateral and longitudinal) and the number on the third stone; throttle → the brightness of the newest ring. Every one of these is a verified field.
- **References:** Suminagashi prints and the way they are made; mathematical marbling (the ink-drop and tine-line maps of Lu, Jaffer and others); `CymaticResonanceField` in this catalog, which also draws rings and must not be mistaken for this dash; `KineticLiquidMetal` for a still liquid surface
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the tray's rim in black lacquer and the three stones as river pebbles, rendered at 4K with the light variants the G-swing needs. ComfyUI: the grain of the lacquer, the faint sheen of still water as a normal map, and a washi paper fibre texture for the labels. Penpot: the board with the rings at gears 1, 4 and 8, combed and uncombed. The ink is geometry, never a picture: each ring edge is a closed line of 192 points put through the maps on the CPU. MSDF atlases for Exo 2 300 and 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 500.
- **Risk:** Fine rings can shimmer on the panel: no two edges closer than 12 px at rest, and edges are drawn as soft bands, not hairlines. Rings round a numeral can read as a target: the stones are black and still, and the gear must pass the blur gate with ten rings on the water. The figure must be a function of what the car is doing now: it is rebuilt every frame from the ring areas, the revs and the force, never from a stored picture, so it cannot smear or drift. Perf: at most ten ring edges of 192 points through three maps each, drawn as 240 line chunks, est. 0.15 ms.
