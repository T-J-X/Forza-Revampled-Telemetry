**One of three liquid-metal directions, built as its own dash** (TJX put it in Abstract, on a card at 08:23, and `OilSlickChrome` in Psychedelic). A dish of liquid metal shaken by the engine. A fluid shaken up and down at frequency f does not answer at f: past a threshold its surface breaks into standing waves at f ÷ 2 (Faraday, 1831), and the cells form squares, hexagons or quasi-crystal patterns. Here the shaking is the engine's firing frequency, so the revs set the wavelength exactly and the throttle sets how hard the pattern stands. Reuse the `KineticLiquidMetal` thread's research notes (Faraday sources) as step 1's start, and check them against sources.

Starting layout, on true black. Rank 4: the dish, Ø760 at (0, −20), standing for a 60 mm dish of a gallium alloy (ρ = 6,440 kg/m³, σ = 0.534 N/m, depth 5 mm), drawn as a height field with true normals that mirrors the studio environment. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 100) under the dish.

- **Signature moment:** The pattern climbs with the revs. The firing frequency of a four-stroke engine is rpm ÷ 60 × cylinders ÷ 2 (four cylinders by default; the packet has no cylinder count, so it is an OSD setting, `FaradayMandalaCylinders`), and the surface answers at half of it. The wavenumber comes from the capillary-gravity dispersion ω² = (g k + σ k³ ÷ ρ) tanh(k h), so at 800 rpm about 3 cells cross the dish and at 9,000 rpm about 21. The pattern is a sum of standing plane waves, Σ cos(k e_i · x + φ_i): squares (two directions 90° apart) in the lowest third of the revs, hexagons (three at 120°) in the middle third and a twelve-fold quasi-pattern (six at 30°) up to the shift rpm, each band crossfading into the next over 300 rpm. This choice of band is the dash's own mapping, which the handover names: in the lab the pattern depends on the forcing, the viscosity and the depth. At the shift rpm the rim ring takes a steady `shift` colour. On the limiter the phases φ_i drift apart and the surface breaks into spatiotemporal chaos, as a Faraday surface does far above its threshold; only the lamp row strobes. The waves are drawn stroboscopically, at the crest of each cycle as Faraday photographs are, because 25 to 300 Hz is faster than any frame rate: only the pattern and its height move on screen.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → the wavenumber, the pattern band, the rim ring, lamps and limiter; throttle → the forcing above the threshold, ε = throttle − 0.05, and the wave height A ∝ √ε as at a supercritical onset, so with the throttle shut the metal is a flat mirror; gear → the numeral on the post; speed → tabular speed; lateral G → the light's azimuth over the metal; longitudinal G → a slow tilt of the dish's mean level on `fluidSlosh`. Idle with the throttle shut is still.
- **References:** Faraday (1831) on the forms of fluids on vibrating surfaces; Benjamin and Ursell (1954) on the stability of the surface (the f ÷ 2 response); Edwards and Fauve (1993) on Faraday patterns and quasi-patterns; Faraday-wave experiments with liquid gallium alloys; `KineticLiquidMetal`, `ChronoMultiverse` and `CymaticResonanceField` in this catalog, so the standing waves do not look like the ferrofluid or the Chladni plate

- **Risk:** A dense pattern at high revs can moiré on the OLED: at most 21 cells across at 4K (36 px per wavelength at 9,000 rpm for four cylinders, capped there for more cylinders), analytic normals and no texture. A busy surface behind the gear can fail the blur gate: the pinned post gives a still disc of 1.6 × the cap round the numeral. Chaos must not flash: the phase drift is smooth and the surface's brightness is capped. Next to `CymaticResonanceField` this must not read as a plate figure: it is liquid metal, reflective, with no sand and no nodal lines drawn. Perf: one pass over the Ø760 disc summing at most 12 cosines with analytic normals, est. 0.1 ms.
