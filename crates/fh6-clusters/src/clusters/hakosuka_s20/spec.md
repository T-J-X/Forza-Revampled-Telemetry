**A twist on the first Skyline GT-R, the "Hakosuka"** (TJX, 2026-11-28: Skyline twists, "some retro and some modern"; in the game as the 1971 Nissan Skyline 2000 GT-R). Recalled, and to confirm in step 1 from period brochures, restoration records and museum cars: two large round dials in deep tubes on a bare black panel, a tachometer scaled to 10,000 rpm for the S20 straight-six with the red zone from about 7,500, a 240 km/h speedometer with a drum odometer in its face, a few small dials turned toward the driver, white numerals on matt black and nothing else, because the GT-R was sold stripped. One page read in the check of 2026-11-28 mentions wood inserts in the GT-R's cabin, so settle the panel's finish from photographs before drawing it. The twist: the dials stay mechanical, and two period racing devices do the modern jobs. A tell-tale needle records each pull, and number drums show the gear and the speed.

Starting layout, detached parts on true black. Rank 3: the tachometer, Ø420 at (+250, −20) in a deep tube with a thin bright ring. Rank 1: the gear on a single white-on-black number drum (Barlow Semi Condensed 700, cap 170) in a window at (0, −40) between the two dials, under a row of 10 small jewelled lamps on a straight line (740 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 2: the speed on three number drums (cap 96) in the face of the left dial, Ø420 at (−250, −20), which keeps a speedometer needle around them. Rank 4: three Ø150 dials at the right, turned 12° toward the driver, for boost, throttle and G. Rank 5: engraved labels in a period face and the badge.

- **Signature moment:** The tell-tale. Racing tachometers of the period carried a second, thin needle that the main needle pushed up and left behind, to show the highest revs reached. Here the tell-tale rides up with every pull and stays at the peak when the driver shifts; it drops back on the `light` spring when the next pull passes 3,000 rpm. A glance after a corner shows exactly where the last shift happened. The number drums turn like the real thing, one clean roll per change on the `snap` spring, and are never left between two numbers. On the limiter only the lamp row strobes.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → tachometer needle (`needle` spring with feed-forward), the tell-tale's peak hold, lamp row and limiter; gear → the gear drum; speed → the three speed drums, held with hysteresis so the last drum does not flutter, and the speedometer needle; boost, throttle and acceleration → the three small dials. Fuel, water temperature, oil pressure and the ammeter have no verified telemetry, so their dials show the three values above, and the handover lists that as a deliberate change.
- **References:** Nissan Skyline 2000 GT-R (PGC10 and KPGC10) instrument panels in period brochures and restored cars; chronometric racing tachometers with a tell-tale needle; mechanical drum counters; `PorscheAnalog` in this catalog for rendered dials and tubes
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: both dial tubes with their rings, faces, needles and hub caps, the number drums as real cylinders with printed numerals (glTF for the kit's mesh pass, with a flipbook fallback), and the jewelled lamp caps. ComfyUI: the matt crackle-black panel paint, the dial-face print with its period numerals, and a worn steering-wheel wood for the environment the rings reflect. MSDF atlases for Barlow Semi Condensed 500 and 700.
- **Risk:** Two equal dials and a small centre window can lose the gear: the drum window is the brightest part of the dash and the dial faces stay under 20% luminance. A rolling drum is slower to read than a digit that snaps: the roll takes at most 120 ms and the new number is fully in place before the eye arrives. `PorscheAnalog` is also a row of tubes, so this dash must read as a 1971 Japanese panel: bare black, bright thin rings, plain numerals, no carbon and no chrome flourish. Perf: two dial plates, four drum meshes of about 400 triangles each, est. 0.25 ms.
