# BoschDdu research: the Bosch Motorsport DDU 10 race display

Step 1 of `program/phases/07-cluster-factory/CLUSTER_PROMPT.md`. Researched on 2026-11-28 from Bosch Motorsport's product pages, data sheets and operation manuals, the makers' own documents for the cars that ship the display, and the licensed sim manuals of those cars (marked as such). Every point names its source. Where no source says something, the gap is listed at the end rather than guessed.

## What this dash recreates

**The Bosch Motorsport DDU 10 ("Display and Data Unit"), as fitted in the BMW M4 GT3.** It is the current flagship of Bosch's two race displays: Bosch's display category lists only the DDU 10 and the smaller DDU 11 ([Bosch Motorsport, Displays](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/products/displays/)), and its Australian distributor calls the DDU 9 "older generation ... phased out in favour of our current generation DDU 10 and 11" ([Bosch Motorsport Shop AU, AMG GT3 Evo](https://www.bosch-motorsport-shop.com.au/amg-gt3-evo)).

- **DDU 10:** a 7-inch colour display, 800 × 480 px, with an ambient light sensor, **20 freely configurable RGB LEDs, 10 across the top and 5 down each side**, IP67, 198 × 134 × 35 mm, 875 g, 12 user pages in page sets ([DDU 10 product page](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/products/displays/display-ddu-10/); [DDU 10 data sheet](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/media/catalog_content/downloads_catalog/pdf_catalog/data_sheet_70827659_display_ddu_10.pdf)).
- **DDU 11:** 6.5 inches, 800 × 480 px, **8 LEDs across the top and 5 down each side**, 203 × 128 × 38.5 mm, 649 g ([DDU 11 product page](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/products/displays/display-ddu-11/); [DDU 11 operation manual](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/media/catalog_content/downloads_catalog/pdf_catalog/operation_manual_323436043_display_ddu_11.pdf), pp. 8 and 44).
- **The car:** BMW's own M4 GT3 brochure lists "Bosch DDU 10" under displays, with the "BMW M Track Cockpit" menu set up "via the display and steering wheel", and shows the unit lit in the car ([BMW M4 GT3 information brochure](https://s3.amazonaws.com/themes.limelightplatform.com/client/bmw-na/files/Other/BMW+M4+GT3+Infobrochure.pdf), p. 7). McLaren's 720S GT3X product guide names the same unit ([McLaren 720S GT3X product guide](https://view.pagetiger.com/csmnvfz/McLaren720SGT3XProductGuide.pdf)), and the licensed Ferrari 296 GT3 sim manual says the car "features a Bosch DDU 10 display" ([Ferrari 296 GT3 manual, sim](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/Ferrari-296-GT3-V4.pdf)). The display is the same unit in every car; only its pages are the team's.

## What makes it recognisable

### The hardware
1. **One rounded housing, black glass face edge to edge.** Bosch's own press image of the DDU 10 (the product page's main picture, [image](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/media/catalog_content/images_catalog/jpg_catalog/9007199325544587_web.jpg), studied at full size in the built-in browser) shows a black glass front that covers the screen and the whole bezel, a gently arched top edge, large rounded top corners, straight sides, smaller bottom corners, and a satin grey aluminium side wall about a quarter of the face's height deep. The BMW brochure photo shows the same light edge in the car (brochure p. 7).
2. **Proportions.** The housing is 198 × 134 mm (1.48:1); a 7-inch 5:3 panel is about 152 × 91 mm, so the screen fills about 77 % of the width and 68 % of the height (data sheet). In the press image the border below the screen is thin, about 10 to 11 % of the face's height ("Motorsport" and the Bosch logo sit directly under the screen), and the LED brow takes the rest, about 20 %: the brow is 1.4 to 1.8 times the chin (the screen's top edge is not visible on the dark page, so the split is read from the 5:3 panel and the glass's bottom edge).
3. **Ten LEDs across the top, in three groups: 2, 6, 2.** In the press image the top row runs along the arched brow with a clear gap after the second and before the ninth LED; each LED is a small horizontal pill behind the glass, about 2.8 % of the face's width; an unlit LED reads as a short grey dash; the row spans about half the face's width (press image; data sheet: "10 on top").
4. **Five LEDs down each side of the screen,** short horizontal pills in the upper half of each side border (press image; data sheet: "5 on each side"). In the press image the lower two on the left and the lower three on the right are lit blue.
5. **Print on the glass:** "Motorsport" bottom left and the Bosch logo bottom right (press image; BMW brochure p. 7 shows "DDU 10" bottom left in the car). A small round ambient-light sensor sits in the right border (press image; data sheet "ambient light sensor").

### The screen (Bosch's own demonstration page)
Both Bosch press images, the DDU 10 above and the DDU 11 ([image](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/media/catalog_content/images_catalog/png_catalog/336956299_web.png)), show the same page, which is how the display is pictured everywhere:

6. **A round rev gauge in the middle:** a dark disc cut flat by a horizon line, a 270° scale from 0 at about half past seven to the top value at about half past four on a hairline ring with major ticks at each thousand and three minor ticks between, numerals in thousands outside the ring, a thick band hugging the ring on its inside that fills clockwise in the accent colour, its head running on inwards as a thin needle, no unlit track on the empty side, and the rpm as a large number in the disc with "rpm" under it (DDU 10: "2174 rpm", cyan arc on a 0 to 6 scale; DDU 11: "7104 rpm", red sweep on 0 to 8).
7. **The gear, large and white, at the bottom centre inside a glowing perspective beam:** a trapezoid of light from a horizon line across the screen to its bottom edge, brightest at its apex and along its two edges and dark between the legs, like a road running away, overlapping the bottom of the rev gauge (DDU 10: "3" in a cyan beam; DDU 11: "4 GEAR" in a red beam).
8. **Four quarter panels round it:** top left "TEMP" with two horizontal bar gauges (oil and water icons, a fine hatch filled to the value, a marker on each bar); bottom left "FUEL" with a ring gauge and the percentage inside; top right "LAP TIMES" with ACTUAL (stopwatch icon, the largest figure), DIFF_LAPTIME (small, in the accent colour) and PREDICTED (in a highlighted box); bottom right "SPEED" with a large number and the unit under it (both press images).
9. **Panel headers:** the top row's caps labels stand on a thin accent-tinted rule that runs from the screen's edge towards the gauge and kicks up at 45° at the gauge end; TEMP is left-aligned, LAP TIMES right-aligned at the screen's right edge; the bottom row's FUEL and SPEED are left-aligned with no rule, under the horizon line (press images).
10. **One accent colour per theme** on black (cyan on the DDU 10 image, red on the DDU 11), white figures, and labels, units and scale numerals nearly as light as the figures (sampled at sRGB 187 to 247 on the DDU 10 image) (press images). RaceCon's elements are boxes, horizontal and vertical bars, large and small gauges in "Classic" and "Modern" styles, values and texts, enumerations, images and alarm icons, and any of them can change colour when its value crosses a limit with hysteresis ([DDU 10 operation manual](https://www.bosch-motorsport.com/media/catalog_content/downloads_catalog/pdf_catalog/operation_manual_70834315_display_ddu_10.pdf)).

### Shift lights and alarms
11. **Shift lights on the top row, from an rpm channel with per-gear thresholds and hysteresis;** the LEDs follow the six-step brightness switch (DDU 10 operation manual §9.5; data sheet).
12. **Real calibrations:** McLaren's lights step "from left to right: Green, Yellow, Orange, Red and then all illuminate in Blue", in steps that let the driver anticipate the blue, and with the pit limiter on the "left and right-hand LEDs flash white" (McLaren product guide pp. 42 and 45). The DDU 11 press image shows the top row graded green, yellow, orange, red from left to right. The licensed BMW M4 GT3 sim manual describes 2 green, 6 yellow, 2 orange, then all red at the shift point, and a magenta pit-limiter flash ([BMW M4 GT3 manual, sim](https://s100.iracing.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/07/BMW-M4-GT3-Manual_V3.pdf), p. 7).
13. **The side columns carry driver aids:** in the BMW M4 GT3 the side LEDs show traction-control intervention, from one green LED up to five red (BMW M4 GT3 sim manual, p. 7).
14. **Alarms are opaque boxes drawn over the page in four fixed alarm areas,** one alarm per area at a time, several rotating every 1.5 s, blinking at a set rate (DDU 10 operation manual; DDU 11 operation manual pp. 70 to 77).
15. **Start-up:** the Bosch logo, then the configured page (DDU 11 operation manual p. 13).

### Type
16. No Bosch document names the display's face; the manuals offer only a font size per element (DDU 10 and DDU 11 operation manuals). In both press images the figures are a plain, low-contrast geometric sans with a flat-based 2 and a straight 7, of medium weight and slightly condensed against the panel, the labels short caps, units lower case ("rpm", "mph", "kph").

## Deliberate changes and why
- **A. No Bosch marks.** The chin's two print zones become the dash's logo zone (the car's logo, bottom right, where Bosch prints its logo) and the name zone (the custom name or the dash's name, bottom left, where "Motorsport" is printed). Every dash shows its car's logo (CLUSTER_PROMPT, "Personalisation"), and the overlay carries no supplier trademarks.
- **B. The gear leads.** The demo page's gear is about a fifth of the screen's height; here it is the largest figure (cap 132, rank 1, `fh6-hmi-design-rules` 3), still inside its beam. The rpm figure is cut to cap 36 so the speed (cap 58) outranks it. The real unit's proportions still set the sizes: the gear, the speed and the LED row stay under the family's `layout.zones` minimums (cap 150, cap 84, a 720 px row), which do not fit a 7-inch race display in its slots (three digits at cap 84 are about 195 px wide against a 156 px panel; a 720 px row does not fit between the brow's corners).
- **C. Honest panels.** The telemetry has no temperatures, fuel or lap times (`docs/kit/README.md` §13), so each quarter keeps its form with data the packet carries: TEMP's two bars show throttle and brake; FUEL's ring shows combined G (full at 1.5 g, a bead at the force's bearing); LAP TIMES' list shows power (the large figure), boost (the small line where DIFF_LAPTIME sits: its label carries the accent tint at the print's light, its value is white, because the accent-coloured value fell under 4.5:1 in the tokens' glance gate at that size) and the car's shift rpm (the boxed line, where PREDICTED sits, printed because it is the car's constant); SPEED stays. Boost reads in psi, the packet's own unit, until the app has a pressure-unit setting.
- **D. Thresholds from the car.** The shift point is the car's limiter times the tokens' shift share (`Signals::shift_rpm`); the telemetry has no per-gear table.
- **E. Side columns.** Traction control is not in the packet, so the right column shows body slip from velocity (`Signals::slip_deg`, labelled derived in the handover), one LED per 2.5° up to five, in `caution`; the left column shows brake pressure in fifths in `warning`, on the same side as the brake bar. Each column keeps one colour (its count carries the level), and both stay dark when nothing is happening.
- **F. No alarm boxes.** There is no oil, water, fuel or tyre source behind an alarm, and the family rule says no lamp without a packet field (`docs/design/families/race-dash.md`).
- **G. The 2027 step.** The real unit's housing is rendered as physical hardware (Blender, with a ComfyUI anodised-aluminium texture) and seen from the driver's eye, the LEDs light through smoked lenses with a tight glow, and the screen keeps OLED black where the real panel shows dark grey: the housing's light rig puts no softbox over the face, so the glass mirrors nothing and reads by its rim line and its lenses.
- **H. LED lenses.** 28 × 10 px against about 22 × 9 on the press image, so a lit lens holds its colour at a glance; the row's span and grouping follow the image.
- **I. Red zone.** A thin red band from the shift point to the scale's end and a longer, thicker limiter tick, which the demo page does not show; the page's arc has no limit, and the band tells where the shift lights will fire.
- **J. Static print at the cap.** Labels, units, scale numerals and rules are static, and the tokens hold static parts under 20 % relative luminance on the OLED (`motion.idle.staticLumaMaxPct`), so they stay grey where the page prints them nearly white; they still clear 4.5:1 on black (0.192 relative luminance, 4.8:1).
- **K. Strobe from the shift point.** The middle six strobe from the shift point, as the shared shift strip and the sibling dashes do; the outer pairs hold the shift lamp steady, so the shift state also reads by count and position, not only by colour. Reduced flash breathes instead of strobing.
- **L. Figure face.** Saira Semi Condensed 500: the page's figures are slightly condensed and of medium weight; regular-width Saira would not fit three speed digits in the panel at cap 58, and the family's Saira Condensed 700/800 is heavier than the page.

## Gaps
- Housing material and finish, glass type and bonding, screen brightness and colour depth: not stated in the DDU 10 or DDU 11 sources; the satin grey edge is read from the press image.
- The real race pages of the BMW M4 GT3 and Ferrari 296 GT3 are known only from licensed sim manuals; no lit race-page photo from BMW, Ferrari or Bosch was found.
- The display's face is not named anywhere.
- The size of an LED lens and the brow-to-chin split are read from the press image only (one yawed image).
