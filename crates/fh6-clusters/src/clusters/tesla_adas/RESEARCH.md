# TeslaAdas research: the Model S and Model X (2021+) driver display

Step 1 of `program/phases/07-cluster-factory/CLUSTER_PROMPT.md`. Researched on 2026-11-27 and 2026-11-28 from Tesla's own owner's manuals, support pages and blog, then press (Not a Tesla App, Electrek, Tesla Oracle, Teslarati, TechCrunch, Motor1, Drive Tesla Canada, TeslaTap, Wikipedia) and owner forums (marked as such). Every point names the page it was read from. The first pass read text only. On 2026-11-28 the positions were then measured from three reference images (listed under "Reference images" below): Tesla's two owner's-manual illustrations of the panel and one press photo of the FSD v14 car model. Anything still marked (est.) was not measured. Where the old brief (`spec.md`) invents something Tesla does not do, the real display wins and the change is listed.

## What this dash recreates

**The 12.3-inch driver display of the refreshed ("Palladium") Tesla Model S and Model X, 2021 to 2026, showing the Full Self-Driving (Supervised) visualisation.**

- **The screen.** The refresh kept a 12.3-inch digital instrument cluster in front of the driver next to a new 17-inch landscape centre screen (2200 × 1300) ([Motor1, "Tesla Model S Updated With Wild New Interior"](https://www.motor1.com/news/482516/new-tesla-model-s-interior-plaid); [Electrek, "Tesla unveils new Model S/X interior"](https://electrek.co/2021/01/27/tesla-unveils-new-model-s-x-interior-crazy-steering-wheel/): "12.3" driver's display", "A second display in front of the driver shows critical driving information"). It is a smaller rectangular screen that "sits more exposed on the dashboard without a prominent hood" ([Go-Parts, "Instrument Cluster Tesla Model S / Model X 2018-2025"](https://www.go-parts.com/garage/instrument-cluster-tesla-model-s-tesla-model-x-2018-2025)). TeslaTap lists the "Gen-4" cluster, "exclusive to Palladium S/X", first released 1 February 2021, as a 12.3-inch IPS LCD at 1920 × 1080, 60 Hz; that resolution is TeslaTap's own reading and no other source confirms it ([TeslaTap, "Instrument Cluster"](https://teslatap.com/articles/instrument-cluster/)).
- **Behind a yoke or a wheel.** The refresh launched with the yoke; Tesla added a round wheel as a factory option on 5 January 2023 and a retrofit for yoke cars ([Electrek, "Tesla round wheel retrofit"](https://electrek.co/2023/01/06/tesla-round-wheel-retrofit/)).
- **The only Tesla with a binnacle driver display.** Model 3, Model Y and Cybertruck have no instrument cluster; speed and battery are on the centre screen. Semi uses two 15-inch screens either side of the driver instead of a cluster ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla Screen Size Comparison for All Tesla Models"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/tesla-reference/1281/SE)).
- **2025 to 2026 changes.** The June 2025 S/X update added a front bumper camera, dynamic ambient lighting and a quieter cabin, and no source reports a change to the driver display ([Tesla Oracle, "2025 Tesla Model S and X refresh include subtle but significant improvements"](https://www.teslaoracle.com/2025/06/16/2025-tesla-model-s-x-refresh-subtle-significant-improvements-exterior-interior-paint-wheels-more/)). The 2025 Spring Update moved the blind-spot (repeater) camera view onto the S/X instrument cluster ([Not a Tesla App, "A Visual Look at Tesla's 2025 Spring Update Features"](https://notateslaapp.com/news/2667/a-visual-look-at-teslas-2025-spring-update-features)). Firmware 2025.20 and later carries Unreal Engine code to replace the Godot engine for the Autopilot/FSD visualisation "only on the AMD-based Model S and Model X" ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla to Switch to Unreal Engine for Improved FSD Visualizations"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3008/tesla-to-switch-to-unreal-engine-for-improved-fsd-visualizationsk)). Tesla announced on 28 January 2026 that S and X production would end in the second quarter of 2026 ([TechCrunch, "Tesla is killing off the Model S and Model X"](https://techcrunch.com/2026/01/28/tesla-is-killing-off-the-model-s-and-model-x/)); Wikipedia gives 13 May 2026 for the last units off the Fremont line ([Wikipedia, "Tesla Model S"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tesla_Model_S)). Tesla keeps updating the cars' software after that (2026 release notes still list Model S and X, for example [Not a Tesla App, "Update 2026.15.6"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/software-updates/version/2026.15.6/release-notes)).

So the "2027 production-grade version" of this display is our extrapolation: the last real state is the 2026 S/X display with FSD v14 visuals, with Unreal Engine visuals on the way.

## What makes it recognisable

### Layout
1. **Two layouts: parked and driving.** Tesla's manual draws the panel in two states ([Model S Owner's Manual, "Instrument Panel"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-29A7E205-A689-41D1-B69C-3AE821CB70E7.html)):
   - **Parked or off:** a car image showing its state (lights on, a door open), the estimated range (or battery %), the estimated outside temperature, alerts, the drive mode (Park, Reverse, Neutral or Drive) and the time of day.
   - **Driving:** "a real-time visualization of the road as seen by the Self-Driving components", which "zooms in or out based on the detected road type". Its numbered callouts are: (1) a map area (location, wiper menu, Auto Shift instructions, the upcoming route), (2) the detected speed limit, (3) the current speed, (4) the set cruise speed, (5) the next navigation instruction, (6) range, (7) surrounding road users "in their corresponding locations", (8) your Model S, (9) the power meter. The Model X page is the same, adding that the speed-limit icon "reflects the style of speed limit signs used in your market region" and that a second, conditional limit can appear ([Model X Owner's Manual, "Instrument Panel"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/modelx/en_eu/GUID-29A7E205-A689-41D1-B69C-3AE821CB70E7.html)).
2. **Where things sit (est.).** Press and owners give the arrangement in words:
   - the visualisation fills "almost the entire screen" except the right side, and the speed is on the right ([Electrek, "Tesla new user interface first look"](https://electrek.co/2021/06/11/tesla-new-user-interface-first-look/));
   - an owner calls the navigation display "left-side" and the energy display "the small bar at the bottom of the screen" ([TMC forum, "2021+ Model S Refresh - Missing/Incomplete Software Features"](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/2021-model-s-refresh-missing-incomplete-software-features.232503/page-46));
   - indicator lights run "along the top of the panel" (manual, above);
   - the drive mode and, in Track Mode, "TRACK" display "above the driving speed" ([Model S manual, "Drive Modes"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-0B3AA701-1A0F-4F66-B2A6-132F5F2677CD.html); [Model S manual, "Track Mode"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-4B41E787-8D69-4338-98B8-2F70A76629A8.html));
   - with a round wheel the rim hides "everything in the upper center part of the display" and partly covers the speed for one owner ([TMC forum, "Seating position and instrument visibility"](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/seating-position-and-instrument-visibility.314742/)).

   Read together: map card on the left third, the road scene and the car in the centre, a right-hand column holding the drive mode, the speed, the speed-limit sign and the set speed, telltales along the top edge, the power meter as a thin bar along the bottom centre.

   **Measured from the manual's driving illustration (R1).** The screen area is about 8 : 3 (wider than 16 : 9). Positions are fractions of the screen's width and height, from its top-left corner:
   - the map fills the left 0 to 35 %, with no card edge: it fades into the scene. The next-turn card sits at its bottom (x 8 to 31 %, y 71 to 88 %) and a small grey row with the battery bar, range and temperature runs under it (y about 92 %);
   - the road scene fills the centre. The vanishing point is at about (53 %, 20 %); the ego car is seen from behind and above at about (50 %, 73 %), about 8 % of the width wide and 20 % of the height tall, so it is small: the road ahead takes most of the scene. Lane lines are thin and grey, and traffic is small and grey;
   - the speed-limit sign is at about (65 %, 12 %);
   - the speed is the largest thing on the screen: bold white digits centred at about (81 %, 52 %), digit height about 16 to 17 % of the screen height, with a small grey unit label under it;
   - the set speed is a small figure at about (92 %, 46 %) with a blue label under it;
   - the drive mode letters sit in a row at about (77 to 80 %, 93 %), the selected letter in blue and the others nearly invisible, with the time of day to their right;
   - the power meter is one thin line along the very bottom, from about x 32 % to 62 %, with a short green segment left of its centre and a dotted right-hand end.

   **Measured from the manual's parked illustration (R2).** The car is drawn large in the centre in a three-quarter front view; telltales sit in the top-left and top-right corners; battery and temperature sit bottom-left; the power line bottom-centre; "P" and the time bottom-right.

   **From the FSD v14 photo (R3).** The car model is glossy with a crisp roof glass and lit tail lamps, on a dark road, with a soft blue glow on the ground under and around it and a blue path band the width of the car leading away from it.
3. **Not customisable.** Owners list "Instrument cluster is not customizable" among the refresh's missing features (TMC wiki thread above).

### The speed, the gear and the speed limit
4. **Speed is the largest figure.** The manual lists it as its own callout next to the speed limit and the set speed (manual, above). No source names the unit label's position or size.
5. **Drive mode, not gears.** The panel shows P, R, N or D ("Pressing the brake pedal shifts the vehicle into the selected drive mode displayed on the instrument panel") ([Model S manual, "Starting and Powering Off"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-87DD5C22-8D11-4E00-8A04-1D198116B859.html)). Shifting is on a touchscreen strip ("swipe up for Drive, swipe down for Reverse"), hidden at highway speed; a blocked shift gives an alert and a chime on the instrument panel; the backup console buttons light their selected letter amber ([Model S manual, "Shifting"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-E9B387D7-AFEF-4AAF-8685-4FE71E09287D.html)). No source describes how the selected letter is lit on the driver display.
6. **Speed limit sign.** Shown when Speed Assist detects a limit; in Display mode "the sign increases in size when you exceed the determined limit", and a blue outline may appear around it; warnings clear after ten seconds or below the limit. The speed offset changes "the number in the gray speed icon" ([Model S manual, "Speed Assist"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-5D3D4014-4E98-45D7-8BBC-F76BCA9CEC05.html)).
7. **Set speed and the steering icon: grey when available, blue when working.** The cruise speed shows in grey when Traffic-Aware Cruise Control is available and "is highlighted with blue text" when cruising ([Model S manual, "Traffic-Aware Cruise Control"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-DA920829-F1FA-44F9-8754-6D914C524A79.html)). The steering yoke (or wheel) icon is grey when Autosteer is available and blue when it is steering ([Model S manual, "Autosteer"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-20F2262F-CDF6-408E-A752-2AD9B0CC2FD6.html)). In FSD v13 the icon moved between point releases (bottom centre, right middle, top corners); from v14.1 a blue glow under the car replaces it as the "FSD is driving" sign ([Tesla Oracle, "Tesla improves FSD v14 driving visualizations to indicate Autopilot in use"](https://www.teslaoracle.com/2025/10/11/tesla-improves-fsd-v14-driving-visualization-to-indicate-autopilot-in-use-video/)). Tesla's own app mirrors the car with a bright blue "Self-Driving" label directly below the speed ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla App Now Shows Live Animation When Using FSD"](https://notateslaapp.com/news/4402/tesla-app-now-shows-live-animation-when-using-fsd)); the manual says "Self-Driving is shown in blue on the touchscreen" ([Model S manual, "Full Self-Driving (Supervised)"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-2CB60804-9CEA-4F4B-8B04-09B991368DC5.html)).

### The power meter
8. **One centre-zero bar.** "During acceleration the bar fills to the right. During deceleration, when you lift off the accelerator, the bar fills to the left in green", showing regenerated energy (Model S instrument panel page). The Model X page calls it "real-time power usage" (Model X page). No source gives the colour of the power (right-hand) fill or a scale.

### The visualisation
9. **Grey world, highlighted priorities.** Other vehicles are grey; a vehicle that is in or will enter your path turns blue and returns to grey once passed; the lead vehicle is a darker grey; a vehicle needing immediate action is red ([Not a Tesla App, "All Tesla FSD Visualizations and What They Mean"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/tesla-reference/636/all-tesla-fsd-visualizations-and-what-they-mean.)). Tesla's wording: "Objects that are highlighted on the visualization represent priorities that Full Self-Driving (Supervised) is actively controlling for" (FSD manual page). Forward Collision Warning highlights the vehicle ahead "in red on the instrument panel" with a chime ([Model S manual, "Collision Avoidance Assist"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-8EA7EF10-7D27-42AC-A31A-96BCE5BC0A85.html)).
10. **Lane lines: white, yellow, blue, red.** Continuous and dashed white lines are white, yellow lines yellow; red marks the road edge and drivable area; the lane fills blue during a lane change (Not a Tesla App reference). With Autosteer the lane edges are blue; Navigate on Autosteer shows "a single blue line in front of Model S" (Autosteer page). Lane Departure Avoidance shows "a blue indicator line"; Emergency Lane Departure Avoidance "highlights the lane marking in red" ([Model S manual, "Lane Assist"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-ADA05DFF-963D-477D-9A51-FA8C8F6429F1.html)). The 2021 "Mind of Car" view also drew red road boundaries and a purple line between oncoming and same-direction traffic ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla's New 'Mind of Car' FSD Visualizations"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/522/teslas-new-mind-of-car-fsd-visualizations-show-brakes-lights-and-much-more)).
11. **The planned path.** Since FSD Beta 10.8 a continuous blue line: darker where the car will accelerate, faded where it will stop (reference above). Since 11.3.1 it is "a much thicker line" showing the space the car will occupy, with **chevrons in the path when slowing whose opacity and speed follow the deceleration**, and a white or black **stopping line** where the car will stop ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla FSD Beta 11.3.1: New Features and Visualizations"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/1254/tesla-fsd-beta-1131-new-features-and-visualizations)). FSD shows "blue arrows in the driving lane" when traffic ahead slows (FSD manual page); the TACC page captions "lane lines highlighted in blue and chevrons showing a slowdown". The 11.3.1 update removed the "FSD Visualization Preview" toggle (11.3.1 article).
12. **Road furniture.** Traffic lights always as three lamps, blue when the car is obeying that light and grey otherwise; stop signs red; stop lines drawn even when not painted, turning red as you approach; crosswalks a solid grey band; cones orange; poles short grey sticks; unknown objects a debris pile; pedestrians animated and flashing red when approached too fast; other vehicles' brake lights lit (reference and 11.3.1 articles; "Mind of Car" article).
13. **Proximity colour steps.** Lane Assist draws "colored lines radiating from the image of your vehicle" on the side of the object: white farthest, then yellow and orange, red closest (Lane Assist page). High Fidelity Park Assist builds grey 3D shapes that turn yellow, orange then red as they get closer ([Not a Tesla App, "First Look at Tesla's New High Fidelity Park Assist"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/1828/first-look-at-teslas-new-high-fidelity-park-assist-video))). The blind-spot warning light itself is a red light in the upper speaker grille, not on the cluster (Lane Assist page).
14. **The ego car is your car.** The car model takes the paint you set in the Colorizer, factory paint by default, "directly in the visualization, both when parked and moving", and can show the car's first-party wheels ([Not a Tesla App, "Change your Tesla's Color and Wheels With Tesla's Colorizer Feature"](https://notateslaapp.com/news/2048/change-your-teslas-color-and-wheels-with-teslas-colorizer-feature)). From FSD v14.1 a "blue color neon-glow" sits under the model while FSD drives, and the model is crisper (Tesla Oracle v14 article); the same glow appeared in Summon visuals in 2026.2.9 ([Tesla Oracle, "Tesla update 2026.2.9 in Europe shows FSD v14-like blue underglow in Summon"](https://www.teslaoracle.com/2026/03/02/tesla-update-2026-2-9-in-europe-shows-fsd-v14-like-blue-underglow-in-summon-visualizations-release-notes/)).
15. **Model fidelity by version.** 2021: 3D models replace boxes, and "brightness/translucency indicate confidence" of the network in each object ("Mind of Car" article). 10.12: seven scaled vehicle classes (reference article). 2024: oncoming cars no longer jitter but pass smoothly ([Drive Tesla Canada, "Tesla fixes four-year old FSD visualization bug"](https://driveteslacanada.ca/news/tesla-fixes-four-year-old-fsd-visualization-bug-video-comparison/)). V12 UI (April 2024): full-screen visualisations show no surrounding environment or curbs ([Not a Tesla App, "First Look at Tesla's V12 User Interface"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/1988/inside-teslas-new-v12-user-interface)). 2025.38 carries 15 more models (ambulance, fire engine, train, tram, school bus, scooter, wheelchair and others), linked to v14 ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla to Add 15 New Visualizations to FSD"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3234/tesla-to-add-15-new-visualizations-to-fsd-photos-video)). Spring 2026 (2026.14): a higher-quality car model with visible paint and glass reflections, a spotlight from above and a studio "park scene" in place of the plain grey backdrop, on the newer Model 3 and Model Y first ([Not a Tesla App, "First Look at Tesla's New Visualizations in Update 2026.14"](https://www.notateslaapp.com/news/3996/first-look-at-teslas-new-visualizations-in-update-202614-video)).

### Track Mode
16. **Model S Plaid Track Mode.** Plaid only, off at every start. "TRACK displays on the instrument panel above the driving speed, and a Track Mode pane appears on the map" with battery and motor status and "a real-time accelerometer"; temperature bars have no colour in the ideal range and turn yellow, then red; low tyre pressure reads red; brake warnings yellow then red. The **G-meter** "graphically displays peak lateral and longitudinal acceleration values in the form of a circular meter", with the drive's history as a shaded area, reset each session. The lap timer shows the real-time delta to the session's fastest lap, then the lap, previous and best times, with the track highlighted blue on the map (Track Mode page). Not a Tesla App's first look adds that the instrument cluster visualisation shows the car's internals (chassis, wheels, brakes, motors, battery) coloured by status, plus a G-force visualisation ([Not a Tesla App, "Tesla adds Track Mode for the Model S Plaid in 2021.44.30"](https://notateslaapp.com/news/669/tesla-adds-track-mode-for-the-model-s-plaid-in-2021-44-30-images-video&sa=U&ved=2ahUKEwiwk-fUhMz2AhUZ8rsIHdh8DgQQFnoECDAQAg&usg=AOvVaw0JgwodIn5dWXwM0LQyg9bJ)); Tesla's blog lists "a vehicle thermals monitor, lap timer, G-meter" ([Tesla, "Introducing Plaid Track Mode"](https://www.tesla.com/en_ca/blog/introducing-plaid-track-mode)).
17. **Model 3 Performance Track Mode (centre screen, for the colours).** Components are green in their ideal range, blue when cold, red when hot; a tyre is blue when under-used and red when peak grip is exceeded; the G-meter is the default card in the Cards area ([Model 3 Owner's Manual, "Track Mode"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/model3/en_sg/GUID-92CA3DCA-EDA3-4243-861E-2C770F5506FD.html); [Tesla, "Track Mode V2"](https://www.tesla.com/support/track-mode)).

### Telltales, signals and start-up
18. **Telltales along the top.** The manual's table: red BRAKE / PARK / seatbelt / airbag; amber ABS, brake booster, stability control (flashing while active), Tow Mode, turtle; green low beam, parking lights, front fogs, "regen limited" bolt; blue high beam and the cold-battery snowflake; grey Vehicle Hold "H" and auto-high-beam standby (Instrument Panel page). In 2024 Tesla recalled about 2.19 million cars by software because the BRAKE, PARK and ABS letters were smaller than 3.2 mm ([Justia, NHTSA recall 24V051000](https://auto-recalls.justia.com/tesla/model-s/2021/24v051000); [Creative Bloq, "Yes, Tesla really just recalled all of its cars over a font"](https://www.creativebloq.com/news/tesla-font)).
19. **Turn signals.** "The corresponding indicator lights up on the instrument panel"; the manual's icon is a green arrow ([Model S manual, "Lights"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-1C209641-AA23-47AC-B0D1-3FE3779CF222.html)). From spring 2025 the repeater camera view can appear on the cluster with the signal (Spring Update article).
20. **Start-up is a bulb check.** "When the car is off and you first press the brake, indicator lights flash briefly along the top of the panel", then those not needed go off (Instrument Panel page). The displays come on when a door opens; the brake press selects the drive mode shown on the panel (Starting page). In Drag Strip mode the panel reads "Peak Performance Ready", "Cheetah Stance Enabled" and "Ready to launch" (Drive Modes page).

### Colour and type
21. **Named colours, no published hexes.** Tesla's manuals name the colours (grey, blue, green, red, amber, yellow, orange, white) but give no values. The only published Tesla hex found is the tesla.com website's primary button blue `#3E6AE1`, with text greys `#171A20`, `#393C41`, `#5C5E62` on white, read from a third-party extraction of the website's styles, not the car ([GitHub, awesome-design-md "tesla/DESIGN.md"](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/VoltAgent/awesome-design-md/664b3e78fd1a298ba11973822da988483256d4b4/design-md/tesla/DESIGN.md)). Appearance can be Dark, Light or Auto, with an optional warmer palette at night ("Reduce Blue Light") ([Model S manual, "Touchscreen"](https://www.tesla.com/ownersmanual/models/en_us/GUID-518C51C1-E9AC-4A68-AE12-07F4FF8C881E.html)); an owner found no separate brightness or appearance control for the driver display ([TMC forum, "Model S refreshed drivers side display brightness"](https://teslamotorsclub.com/tmc/threads/model-s-refreshed-drivers-side-display-brightness.266105/post-6679465)).
22. **Typeface.** No source names the typeface of the 2021+ in-car UI. The 2013 Model S dashboard display used Gotham ([Fonts In Use, "2013 Tesla Model S dashboard display"](https://fontsinuse.com/uses/3997/2013-tesla-model-s-dashboard-display)). tesla.com uses Universal Sans Display and Universal Sans Text (website extraction above), a commercial customisable sans from the foundry Family Type ([Typewolf, "Universal Sans"](https://www.typewolf.com/universal-sans)). A type blog says Tesla has used "clean geometric sans-serifs" across site, configurator and car, Gotham-adjacent historically ([Made Good Designs, "What Font Does the Tesla Model S Use?"](https://madegooddesigns.com/?p=47340)). Neither Gotham nor Universal Sans is OFL.
    - **OFL faces in this repo** (`assets/fonts/fonts.json`, every one OFL-1.1): Saira Condensed, Saira Semi Condensed, Saira (variable width and weight), Titillium Web, Barlow Semi Condensed, Barlow Condensed, Michroma, Chakra Petch, Exo 2, B612 Mono, Noto Sans (variable width and weight), Orbitron, Rajdhani, Oxanium, Teko, Share Tech Mono, Russo One.
    - **Closest match (est.):** none of these is a geometric sans like Gotham. Noto Sans, at full width in its light weights, is the nearest to a neutral, low-contrast UI sans with round digits, so the dash uses it for the speed, the gear and the labels. Montserrat (OFL, not in the repo) is the usual open stand-in for Gotham; adding it would need a ledger entry and is not assumed here.

### Behaviour
23. **What moves.** The scene "zooms in or out based on the detected road type" (Instrument Panel page); chevrons in the path change opacity and speed with the deceleration (11.3.1 article); the speed-limit sign grows when you are over the limit (Speed Assist page); objects' brightness and translucency follow confidence ("Mind of Car" article); since 2024 other cars glide instead of jumping (Drive Tesla Canada); pedestrians flash red and stop lines turn red on approach (reference article); warnings come with a chime and a message, and "TAKE IMMEDIATE CORRECTIVE ACTION!" appears for a likely red-light run (Autosteer page). No source describes how the speed digits change (rolling, cutting or easing) or how the drive-mode letter changes.

## Deliberate changes and why

- **A. The gear leads, the speed follows.** Tesla needs no gear: the panel shows a drive mode and the speed is the biggest number. Our rank order puts the gear and shift light first, so the right-hand column, where Tesla stacks drive mode over speed, holds the gear number large at the top (a game car has gears 1 to 10, reverse and neutral), the speed below it in a lighter weight with its unit (mph or km/h) under it, and Tesla's R · N · D row at the foot with the current letter lit and the others grey. The speed is the second figure, not the first.
- **B. A shift light Tesla does not have.** A single-speed EV has no rpm to watch, but a racing cluster must show the shift point. It is drawn in Tesla's own vocabulary: a thin rounded bar in the power meter's shape, placed directly above the gear and speed column. It fills from both ends towards the centre between the car's shift-start rpm and its limiter: grey, then white, then Tesla blue for the last stage, and red only at the shift point, Tesla's colour for "act now". The limiter follows the family's strobe rule and the WCAG 2.3.1 flash audit.
- **C. The power meter is the rev/power display (rank 3).** Tesla's centre-zero bar stays, driven by `power_watts` against the car's highest seen power. rpm is carried by the shift light, so there is no separate rev ribbon or pod strip (the old brief's ten pill pods, the 4 px power ribbon with a comet, and the gear pill with neighbour ghosts are not Tesla and are dropped).
- **D. Only verified data.** The packet has no speed limit, cruise or Autopilot state, route, map, range, battery, telltales, turn signals, tyre temperatures or object list, so those parts are left out, not faked. The old brief's SLIP/LOCK chips, tyre thermal steps, suspension ride height and wheel-rotation stripes use fields outside the verified set and are dropped until those offsets are checked in the real game.
- **E. Simulated traffic and the road ahead stay honest.** Traffic is a seeded ambient simulation, labelled as such in the OSD, off in the Zen tier, with no distance labels. In Tesla's language blue and red on another car mark what the car is controlling for and what needs action, so on simulated cars they would claim a sensor reading: the cars stay grey (the lead car darker grey), they never turn blue or red, and the proximity lines are not drawn. The road ahead is different, because it is real: it is the path the car is on right now, worked out from its measured lateral acceleration and speed. It is drawn the way Tesla has drawn the car's own path since 11.3.1, as a band the width of the car in the palette's primary (Tesla blue by default, the user's colour otherwise), and it is the feature a Tesla owner recognises first (R3). The chevrons and the stopping line keep Tesla's form and come from the car's real deceleration. The old brief's FSD corner brackets and 3-step blind-spot arcs are not Tesla's and are dropped.
- **F. The green side of the power meter.** Tesla fills left in green for regenerated energy. A petrol car does not regenerate; the left side fills green only when the packet reports negative power (engine braking), and stays empty if the real game never sends a negative value (to be checked against the real game; the golden packets are synthetic).
- **G. No screen, true black.** The real display is a lit rectangle with a dark-mode background. On the OLED, off pixels are `#000000`: no backing panel or screen outline is drawn, each part floats, and only the road surface is a dim plane (about 6 % luminance or less (est.)) that fades out at its edges instead of ending at a frame.
- **H. The ego car and its glow.** Tesla draws your own car in your own paint. The game car can be any brand and no licensed body can be shown, so the ego is one generic coupe, rendered in Blender in a white pearl paint with a glass roof (the look of R1 and R3), with its brake lamps lit while braking. Tesla's v14 glow under the car means "FSD is driving", which the game cannot know. The glow is too much a part of the 2026 look to drop, so it is kept and given a job the driver needs: it is the shift light's second voice. It is off below the shift-start rpm, grows through the shift stages in the palette's primary, and takes the shift colour at the shift point, steady, never flashing, so the stage reads in peripheral vision from the largest object on the screen. The old brief's amber and violet underglow states are not Tesla's and are dropped. The camera pulls back as speed rises, standing in for Tesla's zoom by road type.
- **I. Start-up.** Tesla's start-up is a flash of the telltale row. The dash has no telltales, so on race start the shift-light segments sweep once and go out, the same bulb-check idea in the part that exists.
- **J. The logo.** No source shows a logo on Tesla's driver display. Each dash shows the driven car's real logo, or the custom name, small and grey at the top of the left card, where it never competes with the gear.
- **K. Track Mode as the default card.** The left map card has nothing to show without a map, so it holds Tesla's Track Mode G-meter: a circle with the current point and the shaded history of peak lateral and longitudinal G, from the verified acceleration. The card carries the Track Mode name as its label (in Japanese over English, see N), where Tesla prints "TRACK" above the speed.
- **L. Type.** Tesla's typeface is not OFL and is not confirmed for the 2021+ car, so Noto Sans (OFL) is used for every figure and label; the user's display font replaces it.

## Japan-inspired: the Wangan at night (TJX, 28 Nov 2026)

TJX asked for the dash to be Japan-inspired because Horizon 6 is set in Japan. The reading taken: it stays Tesla's driver display, as a Japan-market car would show it on the Bayshore Route at night. Japan goes into the road, the traffic, the braking marks and the labels. The layout, the rank order and the palette do not change, and nothing Japanese is the only carrier of a reading.

### What was looked up
- **The game's Japan.** Tokyo is on the map with the C1 Inner Loop and the Bayshore (Wangan) route, Daikoku Parking Area, and touge roads such as Mt. Haruna, Hakone Turnpike and Izu Skyline ([AllThings.How map guide, 18 Apr 2026](https://allthings.how/forza-horizon-6-map-every-confirmed-region-road-and-poi-in-horizon-japan/), an unofficial list). PC Gamer's preview has the Shuto Expressway threading "through the sky" of Tokyo among vertical neon ([PC Gamer, 8 Apr 2026](https://www.pcgamer.com/games/racing/forza-horizon-6s-japan-map-is-the-series-best-yet)).
- **Expressway lane lines.** The lane boundary line is 8 m of white and a 12 m gap, 20 m a set, five sets to about 100 m ([NEXCO East, Drive Plaza](https://www.driveplaza.com/safetydrive/mamechishiki/017.html); the same figures from NEXCO Central in [Traffic News, 27 Jan 2026](https://trafficnews.jp/post/627253)). Drivers are taught to count them for following distance.
- **Delineators.** Round orange reflectors on posts at the road edge, every 50 m on expressways outside curves, also used to judge distance ([Kuruma News, 4 Jun 2023](https://kuruma-news.jp/post/652664)).
- **Signs.** Expressway guide signs are dark green with white text and ordinary-road guide signs dark blue; Latin text is set in Vialog since 2014; the stop sign is a red inverted triangle with 止まれ ([Wikipedia, "Road signs in Japan"](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Road_signs_in_Japan)). Guide signs carry Japanese above and Latin below, which is the model for the labels here.

### What it changes
- **M. The road is the Bayshore Route.** Three lanes, the ego car in the middle one. Lane lines are drawn to the NEXCO figure, 8 m on and 12 m off, with solid white edge lines, and a round orange reflector stands on each edge every 50 m. Japan drives on the left and keeps left, so the seeded traffic is slowest in the left lane and fastest in the right, the passing lane. Traffic is two fastbacks and a kei box (the light-car class: a tall, narrow one-box), each with a 330 × 165 mm plate, white for ordinary cars and yellow for the kei, with the two green rows as plain blocks and no readable number. The ego car gets the same white plate and a ducktail. All of it is still scenery from a seed (E), not sensor data.
- **N. Two-language labels.** Labels are set as Japanese guide signs are: Japanese above, Latin below and smaller. トラックモード over TRACK MODE on the G-meter card, 回転数 over RPM and 出力 over POWER on the status row. Digits, units and the R · N · D row stay Latin, as on a Japan-market car. The Japanese is Noto Sans JP Medium (OFL), which shares its Latin design with the dash's Noto Sans (L).
- **O. Braking is written on the road.** Under braking Tesla draws a stop line. Here the predicted stop point (from measured speed and deceleration) carries a white stop bar with 止まれ painted before it, stretched along the road as road text is, and the ego lane's edges grow the short inner dashes Japanese roads use to slow traffic, more solid the harder the measured braking. Both are true readings in Japanese road paint.
- **P. Not done on purpose.** No speed-limit roundel, route number or destination names: the packet has none of them, and a number on the road would read as a limit (D). No cherry blossom, torii or flag: the game itself holds back on them, and the display stays a car's instrument.
