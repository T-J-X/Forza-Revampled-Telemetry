**A real kaleidoscope, with the mirrors set by the gear.** Two mirrors that meet at an angle of 180° ÷ n show a wedge of loose coloured glass 2n times round a centre, and the copies close without a seam only when n is a whole number. Here n is the gear, so the figure has twice as many sectors as the gear: a mirror image in first, a cross in second, twelve sectors in sixth. The chips in the wedge hang and swing with the force the driver feels, and the mirrors turn that into a figure that breathes through every corner. Step 1 researches real kaleidoscopes (mirror systems, object chambers, how much light a mirror loses) and names its sources.

Starting layout, stacked on the centre line. Rank 1: ten lamps on a straight line at y −300 (760 × 28), filling outside-in, and the gear (Exo 2 800, cap 150) on a true-black core, Ø190, at the middle of the figure. Rank 4: the figure, Ø380 at (0, −30), seen as through the eyepiece. The wedge between the two mirrors holds eight chips of coloured glass, each on its own pivot. Rank 3: a thin arc round the eyepiece, radius 215, 240° over the top from 8 o'clock to 4 o'clock, lit from the left, with numerals outside it; it sits outside the mirrors and is never copied. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 90) to the right of the figure at (+390, −30); to the left, at (−390, −30), the felt force as a number. Rank 5: a thin knurled eyepiece ring and Barlow Semi Condensed labels.

- **Signature moment:** The turn of the mirrors. On every gear change the mirror angle goes from 180° ÷ n to 180° ÷ (n ± 1): the old figure fades over 150 ms and the new one opens with two more sectors, or two fewer. Between changes the chips do the work. Each chip is a damped pendulum of its own length, from 0.25 m to 1 m, so it swings at its own pace, between 1 Hz and 0.5 Hz. In a corner every chip leans to the same angle, atan(lateral g), but the short ones get there first, so the figure fans open and settles. Where two chips overlap, their colours multiply as stacked filters do. Each bounce off a mirror costs 8% of the light, so the copies dim toward the far side of the figure, as in a real instrument. At the shift point the arc reaches its shift mark and holds a steady `shift` colour. On the limiter only the lamps strobe.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max with the car's per-gear thresholds → the arc, the lamps and the limiter; gear → the numeral and the mirror angle (neutral and reverse use one flat mirror, n = 1); speed → the tabular speed; lateral acceleration → the angle the chips hang at; longitudinal acceleration → how far each chip slides along its arm; throttle → the brightness of the light behind the chips. Every one of these is a verified field.
- **References:** Brewster's kaleidoscope (1817) and two-mirror instruments with an object chamber; front-surface mirrors and what they lose at each bounce; stacked colour filters; `HarmonographDrive` in this catalog for pendulums driven by the car
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the eight chips as cut glass with bevels, baked to normal and thickness maps at 4K, and the eyepiece ring in knurled black metal. ComfyUI: seeds, bubbles and striae inside the chips as a labelled grain map, and the dim studio the mirrors reflect. Penpot: the board with the figure at gears 1, 2, 3 and 6. The three chip colours are absorptions built from the palette's primary, secondary and accent, so the driver's palette recolours the figure. MSDF atlases for Exo 2 300 and 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 500.
- **Risk:** A bright, symmetric figure round the gear competes with it: no chip is brighter than 35% of the gear, the core is true black, and the gear and the lamps must pass the blur gate with the figure at full light. Nothing in the figure may flicker: the chips swing at 1 Hz or slower, and the change of mirrors is a fade, never a cut. A figure of sectors is close to `VisionPetals`: this one is made of mirror copies of loose chips, and its sector count is twice the gear. Perf: at most 20 copies of eight chips and one arc, all instanced, est. 0.15 ms.
