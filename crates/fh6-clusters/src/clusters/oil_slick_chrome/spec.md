**One of three liquid-metal directions, built as its own dash.** `KineticLiquidMetal` keeps the ferrofluid crown and `FaradayMandala` the standing waves; this one is a drop of liquid chrome wearing a film of oil, whose rainbow is real thin-film interference. Reuse the `KineticLiquidMetal` thread's research notes (thin film, liquid-metal optics) as step 1's start, and check them against sources.

Starting layout, on true black. Rank 4: the blob, a 2D metaball surface F(x) = Σ q_i² ÷ |x − c_i|² with the skin at F = 1, about 900 × 620 at (0, −20), shaded as a height field with true normals; its body is liquid chrome reflecting the studio environment, and over it lies an oil film whose colour is computed, never painted. Rank 3: the tach, a chrome ribbon that pours along a 260° arc (R 470) round the blob and joins it by smooth union where it lands, its tip the exact rpm. Rank 1: the gear (Exo 2 800, cap 200) seen through a void at the blob's heart, made by one negative charge so the metal flows round the numeral; 11 droplet beads on a straight line above the blob (720 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 104) under the blob, 420 px from the gear.

- **Signature moment:** The film drains. The oil runs down the skin under the car's own G: its thickness follows Jeffreys' law for a draining film, h = √(μ s ÷ (ρ g_eff t)), where s is the distance down the skin along the felt G, g_eff the felt acceleration and t the time since the film was last renewed. So the colours are banded across the blob in Newton's order, slide toward the side the car is pushed, and slowly climb down the orders at a steady cruise. A throttle stab renews the film (t resets) and the bands flood back in. At the shift rpm the ribbon's last tenth takes a steady `shift` colour; on the limiter only the bead row strobes, and the ribbon's tip spills droplets into the blob that merge by smooth union. On an upshift the void closes over the old numeral and reopens round the new one in 120 ms.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → ribbon length on the `ribbon` spring with feed-forward, beads and limiter; gear → the void's numeral; speed → tabular speed; longitudinal and lateral G → g_eff and the drainage direction, and the blob's centres c_i on `fluidSlosh` springs; throttle → film renewal (a rise of 0.3 within 200 ms resets t) and the blob's charge q, so the metal swells under power; brake → the blob flattens toward the bottom by the deceleration. Idle is still: no input, no motion, and the film stops at its thinnest order.
- **References:** thin-film interference and Newton's colour series; Jeffreys (1930) on the draining of a film from a vertical plate; Blinn's metaballs (1982); oil films on water and on molten metal; `docs/specs/optics.md` §9 for the film; `KineticLiquidMetal` and `ChronoMultiverse` in this catalog, so the three liquid-metal dashes do not look alike
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the studio environment the chrome reflects (a 4K HDR panorama with soft boxes), and the droplet bead caps. ComfyUI: none draws the figure (colour law rule 5); a faint caustic plate for the black under the blob. A 256 × 64 baked look-up table of the film's reflectance against thickness and angle, computed from §9 at load. MSDF atlases for Exo 2 300 and 800.
- **Risk:** A rainbow blob can swamp the frame: the film colour lives only on the rank-4 figure (colour law rule 3), at most 60% of the blob's lit area is saturated, and the gear's void keeps a feathered dark pad so the numeral passes the blur gate. A metaball can pinch the void shut: the negative charge keeps a minimum radius of 1.3 × the numeral's cap. Thin-film colour on a metal needs the metal's complex index: §9 handles a film in air, so the thread adds a metal substrate to `optics.md`, with its source, before using it. Perf: one SDF pass over about 1000 × 700 with at most 8 charges, a LUT fetch per pixel, est. 0.15 ms.
