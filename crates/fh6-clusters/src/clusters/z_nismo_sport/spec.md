**A twist on the Nissan Z Nismo, old pods over a new screen** (TJX, 2026-11-28: Skyline and Nismo twists, "some retro and some modern"; in the game as the 2024 Nissan Z Nismo). Found in Nissan's press kits by the check of 2026-11-28 (read them again in step 1): a 12.3-inch screen with Normal, Enhanced and Sport modes, the redline shift point at 12 o'clock, and a Sport mode that puts the tachometer in the centre with a shift-up light at the top; a red outline round the Nismo's tachometer; and three analogue pod gauges on top of the dash for boost, turbo speed and voltage, which quote the three pods of the 1969 240Z. The twist: the two eras are built as what they are. The screen is a screen, and the three pods are rendered as physical 1969 hardware standing above it.

Starting layout, detached parts. Rank 3: the Sport-mode tachometer, a Ø440 drawn dial at (0, +10) turned so that the red zone ends at 12 o'clock, with a red outline, scaled from `EngineMaxRpm`. Rank 1: the gear (Saira Condensed 800, cap 180) in the dial centre and a shift-up bar of 10 cells on a straight line at the top of the screen part (740 × 28), filling outside-in. Rank 2: tabular speed (Saira Condensed 600, cap 96) to the left of the dial, 380 px from the gear. Rank 4: three Ø170 pods in deep hooded tubes on a line above the dial's shoulders at y −230, turned toward the driver, for boost, power and G, with real needles. Rank 5: the Nismo wordmark with its red final letter as the logo zone.

- **Signature moment:** Redline at noon. In Sport mode the needle climbs clockwise to the top, so the moment to shift is always straight up, where the eye already is. At the shift rpm the shift-up bar is full and the dial's red outline brightens to a steady `shift`; on the limiter only the bar strobes. The three pods answer in their own time, as mechanical needles do: the boost needle swings up on the `needle` spring and overshoots a little on a hard pull, while the screen below it is instant. Two eras, two motions, one dash.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max → tachometer needle, shift-up bar and limiter; gear → centre numeral; speed → tabular speed; boost → the first pod; power → the second; acceleration (the larger of lateral and longitudinal, signed) → the third. Turbo speed and voltage, which the real pods show, are not in the telemetry: the two pods are re-labelled for power and G, and the handover lists that as a deliberate change. The display mode is fixed to Sport; Normal and Enhanced are not built.
- **References:** Nissan Z and Z Nismo (RZ34) press kits and owner videos of the three display modes; the 240Z's three dash-top gauges; the Nismo logotype in `assets/logos/`; `HakosukaS20` in this catalog, which shares the period-pod method
- **Phase 6 assets:** Blender: the three pods as real hardware (hooded tubes, faces, needles, hub caps and glass) with three light azimuths, and the screen's thin frame. ComfyUI: the pods' crackle-black finish and face print in the 240Z's style, and a stitched dash-top surface for them to stand on. MSDF atlases for Saira Condensed 600 and 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 500. Procedural fallback for every part.
- **Risk:** Three pods above the gear can crowd the shift-up bar: the bar sits on the screen part under the pods' line, and the pods stay at rank 4 luminance with no glow. A turned scale is unfamiliar: number it clearly and keep idle at the lower left. A deliberate lag on the pod needles must stay small (the kit's `needle` spring, nothing slower), so no reading is ever late enough to mislead. Red is the Nismo colour and also the warning colour: the outline and the wordmark count against the red budget, and nothing red flashes. Perf: one dial, three pod plates and needles, est. 0.2 ms.
