**Drops of clear glass that run together and pull apart.** Apple's Liquid Glass, shown in 2025, lets controls merge and separate like drops of liquid. That is recalled, not checked, because the search budget had run out: step 1 confirms it from Apple's own material. This dash is made of nothing else: every part is a drop, and the drops join and part with the car. Nothing here is a plate, which is what sets it apart from `LiquidGlass`. The kit already has the tool: pieces in one group share one outline, the smooth union of `docs/specs/optics.md` section 2.

Starting layout, three drops in a row over OLED black, with every light under the glass. Rank 1: the gear (Saira Condensed 700, cap 160) under a round drop, Ø250 at (0, −30), and above it the shift lights: ten drops, Ø34, on a straight line at y −292 with 81 px between centres (763 px over all). Rank 2: tabular speed (Saira 300, cap 90) under a long drop, 280 × 150 at (+330, −30). Rank 4: the same drop mirrored at (−330, −30) shows power, with the peak of the current pull held. Rank 3: a clear channel, 1100 × 36 at y +170, with the rev light running under it from the left and etched ticks. Rank 5: Titillium Web labels etched into the channel, and the logo zone. The numerals never move: each stands on a fixed black core, and only the glass round it flows.

- **Signature moment:** The thread. As each shift stage lights, the lit drops run together from both ends, and at the shift point the ten have become one liquid thread 18 px thick. On an upshift the thread breaks back into ten drops, and the sizes are those of a real liquid thread (Rayleigh–Plateau): a thread of radius 9 px breaks every 9.01 radii, 81 px, into drops of 1.89 radii, 17 px. On the limiter only the thread strobes. The three large drops do the same thing slowly: the blend length of their smooth union follows the car's longitudinal G, from 20 px under braking of 0.5 g to 60 px under acceleration of 0.3 g. Their surfaces are 65 px apart, and two surfaces join when that gap is 1.386 times the blend length, so under power the row is one body and under braking it is three.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max with the car's per-gear thresholds → the channel's light, the drops, the thread and the limiter; gear → the numeral; speed → the tabular speed; power → the left drop, with the peak held until the next pull begins; longitudinal acceleration → the blend length of the three drops; lateral acceleration → the studio light that swings across every rim, and a sideways lean of the two outer drops' glass of at most 12 px. Every one of these is a verified field.
- **References:** Apple's Liquid Glass (2025), where controls merge and part; the Rayleigh–Plateau break-up of a liquid thread; two drops joining; `LiquidGlass` in this catalog for the optics, and `KineticLiquidMetal` for a liquid's free surface; `docs/specs/optics.md` section 2 for the smooth union
- **Phase 6 assets:** Procedural: the kit's clear optical glass with merged outlines; no texture is painted inside the glass. Blender: a normal and thickness pair for a sessile drop at 4K, for the three large drops. ComfyUI: the studio panorama the drops reflect, and a soft caustic for the light each drop throws on the black beneath it. Penpot: the board with the row as one body and as three. MSDF atlases for Saira 300, Saira Condensed 700 and Titillium Web 600.
- **Risk:** Glass with no straight edge can read as a grey smudge on an OLED: the rims come from the kit's Fresnel, the light is under the glass, and every numeral keeps its black core and must pass the blur gate through the glass. Glass that flows next to a numeral distracts: the cores stay still, and the necks form between the drops, never over a digit. The thread must keep the strip's 720 px span and its height of at most 42 px in both states. If one group cannot take the thirteen pieces this dash needs, stop and report it. Perf: thirteen pieces in two groups and one channel, est. 0.3 ms.
