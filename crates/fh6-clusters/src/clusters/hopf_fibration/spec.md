**A torus woven from linked rings, each one an exact circle.** The Hopf fibration fills the three-sphere with circles, one for every point of an ordinary sphere. Seen in ordinary space each circle stays a circle, any two of them are linked exactly once, and the circles over one circle on the sphere weave a torus. Tilting that circle bends the torus into a cyclide, a ring that swells on one side. Step 1 takes the formulas from a textbook source and checks them against the values in the maths table. The car drives the two things a driver can read from it: how fat the torus is, and which way it leans.

Starting layout. Rank 1: ten lamps on a straight line at y −300 (760 × 28), filling outside-in, and the gear (Exo 2 800, cap 150) at (0, −180), above the figure on true black. Rank 2: tabular speed (Exo 2 300, cap 84) to the right of the gear at (+260, −180); to the left, at (−260, −180), the felt force as a number. Rank 4: the figure, seen from 25° above its plane, centred at (0, +70) and zoomed so that it always fits a box of 720 × 330: sixteen rings, the fibres over sixteen evenly spaced points of one circle on the sphere. Near parts of a ring are brighter and wider than far parts, and each ring is drawn over a black edge, so the eye sees which ring passes in front at every crossing. Rank 3: the front half of the figure's outer rim is a lit arc with ticks, from 9 o'clock down through 6 to 3 o'clock, with Barlow Semi Condensed numerals inside it. Rank 5: micro-labels and the logo zone.

- **Signature moment:** The lean. The circle's angle from the pole follows the revs, from 40° at idle to 110° at the shift point, so the torus grows from a thin band (tube 0.36, ring 1.06, in units of the centre circle) to a fat one (tube 1.43, ring 1.74), and the view zooms out so that it keeps its size. The felt force tilts the circle on the sphere, 25° for each g, toward the side the car is pushed: the torus swells on that side and thins on the other, and every ring is still an exact circle. With steady revs and no force, nothing moves. On an upshift every ring slides one place round the torus, a turn of 22.5° of the sphere about the circle's own axis, over 300 ms on the `snap` spring: the weave visibly turns and the torus keeps its shape. On the limiter only the lamps strobe, and the rim arc holds a steady `shift` colour.
- **Telemetry:** rpm, idle and max with the car's per-gear thresholds → the circle's angle from the pole, the rim arc, the lamps and the limiter; gear → the numeral, and the one-place slide on a change; speed → the tabular speed; acceleration → the tilt of the circle (lateral and longitudinal), the number beside the gear and a parallax of 4 px per g between the near and far halves; throttle → the brightness of the rings. Every one of these is a verified field.
- **References:** The Hopf fibration and its stereographic picture; Villarceau circles and Dupin cyclides; knot diagrams, for the gap that shows which strand passes over; `GeodesicWell` in this catalog for geometry placed in space
- **Phase 6 assets:** Procedural: every ring is 48 points of the closed form, put through the turn, the projection and the camera on the CPU, and drawn as line chunks sorted far to near. ComfyUI: a soft glow sprite for the near side of a ring and the dim studio it catches. Blender: none. Penpot: the board with the torus thin, fat and leaning. MSDF atlases for Exo 2 300 and 800 and Barlow Semi Condensed 500.
- **Risk:** Sixteen thin rings can read as a wire drawing, below the bar of a production display: rings have width, depth shading and a soft glow, and the direction check asks whether it looks made, not plotted. A dense figure under the gear pulls the eye: no ring is brighter than 35% of the gear, and the gear and speed stand clear of it on black. The fibre through the far pole is a straight line that runs off the screen: the angle stops at 110°, and the tilt is limited so that no ring comes within 15° of that pole. Perf: 16 rings of 48 segments, each drawn twice (edge, then light), 192 line chunks, est. 0.2 ms.
